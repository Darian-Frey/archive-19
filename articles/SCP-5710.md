# SCP-5710

**Item #:** SCP-5710

**Object Class:** Euclid (provisional — see Addendum 5710-H)

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-5710 is accessed through a fire door at the eastern end of the Sub-Level 2 corridor of Site-██, an administrative site with no prior containment role. The door is a standard Foundation fire door. It carries the correct signage. It is not on the building's construction drawings, and no member of Site-██ facilities staff installed it.

The door is kept closed and alarmed. Entry is restricted to **Project Threshold** personnel under a rota, and each individual's cumulative exposure to SCP-5710 is limited to six hours per calendar month. No individual is to proceed beyond Room 17. Personnel are not to enter any room that has begun to *resemble them* (Addendum 5710-F). Personnel are not to bring personal effects into SCP-5710. Personnel are not to touch anything that has not yet finished becoming what it is becoming.

The rooms nearest the door are inspected weekly against the Project Threshold external record (Addendum 5710-D). When a room is found to have been incorporated into Site-██ — that is, when it appears on the building's drawings, is assigned a room number by Site administration, and is remembered by non-Project staff as having always been there — it is quietly reassigned to Project Threshold as office space and the door's designated position is advanced to the next room inward. This has occurred four times.

Site-██ facilities staff have been informed that the eastern corridor is subject to "ongoing structural survey." They accept this. Two of them have, on separate occasions, mentioned to Project staff that the eastern corridor "was always a bit odd," and that they "never liked the old annexe." There is no old annexe. There was not, until 20██, an eastern corridor.

A worldwide survey for further instances is in progress (Addendum 5710-G). It is not expected to conclude.

---

**Description:** SCP-5710 is a corridor, and the rooms opening from it, located beyond the eastern fire door of Site-██ Sub-Level 2. The corridor is approximately 2.4 metres wide and of indeterminate length. It is lit. The lighting is, in the near rooms, standard Foundation fluorescent fitting; in the far rooms, it is not lighting in any sense the Foundation can describe, and the rooms are illuminated anyway.

The rooms of SCP-5710 are arranged in a gradient. The nearest rooms are indistinguishable, or very nearly indistinguishable, from ordinary Foundation office space. Each successive room is *less* so. By approximately Room 10, the rooms are recognisable as rooms only in the loosest sense: enclosed volumes with something like a floor, containing objects that are something like furniture, marked with something like writing. By Room 17, the resemblance to any human interior is faint. Beyond Room 22, the corridor is not describable, and personnel who have attempted to describe it agree only that it is not dark.

The rooms are changing. Every room in SCP-5710 is, on every inspection, slightly more like an ordinary room than it was on the previous inspection. The far rooms are becoming middle rooms. The middle rooms are becoming near rooms. The near rooms are becoming Site-██.

The Foundation's working characterisation, adopted after eighteen months of observation and stated here without qualification because no qualification has survived contact with the evidence, is as follows:

**SCP-5710 is another reality forgetting itself. As it forgets, it remembers ours instead. The rooms are the memory, and the memory is converging on us.**

The Foundation does not know what the other reality was. It has fragments (Addendum 5710-E). It knows that the fragments are being erased, room by room and month by month, by the steady advance of things it recognises: doors, desks, fluorescent tubes, fire signage, a photocopier. It is watching something become an administrative annexe. It is not certain the thing was ever anything else. It is fairly certain it was.

Project Threshold personnel refer to SCP-5710 as **the Front Rooms**. The designation originated as a joke, by reference to the online "Backrooms" creepypasta, and has persisted because no one has proposed a better one and because, as the Project lead observed in the second quarterly report, "it is not actually a joke."

## Addendum 5710-A — Discovery

SCP-5710 was reported on ██/██/20██ by Mr. ████ K████, a facilities electrician at Site-██ with twenty-two years' service, in a maintenance ticket reproduced here in full:

> *Sub-2 east corridor. There's a fire door at the end that I didn't put there and I'd know. It's a proper door — correct spec, correct signage, closer works, alarm contact wired in and reporting to the panel. Panel calls it FD-2E-07. There's no FD-2E-07 on the schedule. There's no 07. The corridor ends at 06. Went and looked at the drawings, corridor ends at 06 on the drawings too. Door's still there. Haven't opened it. Please advise.*

The ticket was routed, correctly, to Site security, who opened the door.

Behind it was a corridor of ordinary appearance, with a room on the left. The room contained a desk, a chair, a filing cabinet, a wall-mounted whiteboard, and a Foundation-standard telephone. The telephone was not connected to anything. The desk drawers contained nothing. The whiteboard was clean, but the security officer who first entered reported that it "looked like it had been recently wiped, by someone who had used it for a long time." A second room, further along, was similar but *less* so: the desk was the right height but the wrong shape, the chair had a back on more than one side, and the whiteboard was a whiteboard-sized region of the wall that was smoother than the rest of it.

The security officer proceeded as far as the fourth room, reported that she was "no longer sure what she was looking at," and withdrew.

Mr. K████ was interviewed and asked whether he had noticed anything unusual about the corridor prior to the door's appearance. His response is recorded:

> *No. It's a corridor. But I'll tell you what I did notice, thinking back. The corridor's always been a bit long for what's on it. Six doors, and it goes on a bit past the last one, and there's nothing there — no cupboard, no riser, no reason for it. I've walked past that bit of wall probably four thousand times and I've never once thought about it. I'm thinking about it now.*

Site-██ construction drawings, checked against the original 1987 build contract, show the eastern corridor as terminating at Room 06. They show no dead-end extension. The extension is approximately eleven metres long. It has, according to Mr. K████ and to every other long-serving staff member interviewed, "always been there."

The Foundation regards this as the first instance of what it later came to call *incorporation*, and notes that it had already happened before the door was found.

## Addendum 5710-B — Survey

Project Threshold was established within a month of discovery and conducted the first systematic survey of SCP-5710 between ██/██ and ██/██/20██. Twenty-two rooms were catalogued. The survey team was withdrawn at Room 22 by unanimous request. Summary descriptions of representative rooms, as recorded at the time of the first survey, follow. They are no longer accurate. Nothing in SCP-5710 stays as described.

**Room 1.** A Foundation office. Indistinguishable, at first survey, from a Site-██ office in every respect except that its telephone was not connected and its light switch operated a light in Room 2.

**Room 3.** A Foundation office in most respects. The carpet was carpet but had no pile direction. The wall clock had the right face and hands, and the hands moved at the right rate, but the numerals were not numerals — they were marks that had the *density* of numerals. The room had a distinct smell that three surveyors independently described as "photocopier toner" and a fourth as "rain."

**Room 6.** Recognisably an office. The desk was a slab supported at four points that were not legs. The chair had a seat and a back and could be sat in, though no surveyor did. Writing on the wall, in a script no one could read, was arranged in a layout that strongly resembled a fire evacuation notice.

**Room 9.** A room. Something that was not a desk stood where a desk would stand. Light came from the upper surfaces of objects, not from fixtures. The floor was not level, and was not un-level either; surveyors reported that the sense of "downwards" was reliable but that the floor did not entirely agree with it. There was writing on the floor.

**Room 12.** An enclosed volume. The walls met at angles that surveyors could not agree on. Objects were present. They had *places* — the survey team reported a strong sense of where one was supposed to stand, sit, or put things, without any object that could be identified as a place to do so. The room had something like weather. It was not cold.

**Room 15.** Surveyors were able to enter, remain for approximately four minutes, and withdraw, and were able to agree on the following: it was inside; it was inhabited, or had been; and it was becoming somewhere.

**Room 19.** Contains the object designated **5710-19-A** (Addendum 5710-E).

**Room 22.** The last room the survey team entered. The survey team's description consists of a single sentence, agreed by all four members: *"It is not finished being anything yet."*

## Addendum 5710-C — Convergence

Project Threshold has inspected SCP-5710 at weekly intervals since the first survey. The changes are consistent in direction, cumulative, and irreversible.

**Every room becomes more like a room.** Room 9's not-desk now has legs. Room 6's evacuation notice has, over fourteen months, resolved from unreadable script into Latin letters spelling nothing, and then into Latin letters spelling **AUTHORISED PERSONNEL ONLY**, in Foundation-standard signage typeface. Room 3's clock has numerals. Room 12's weather has stopped.

**The change is not addition.** The Foundation initially modelled convergence as the rooms *acquiring* features of Foundation offices. Close observation has established that they are *losing* features of something else, and that what remains, when the something else is gone, happens to be an office. The not-desk did not grow legs. It lost whatever it had instead of legs, and the loss left legs. The evacuation notice did not learn English. It forgot what it was saying, and what was left over was the shape of an English sentence.

**The rate is not constant.** Rooms that Project personnel enter regularly converge faster than rooms observed only by camera. Rooms observed only by camera converge faster than rooms not observed at all. Rooms not observed at all still converge. The Foundation is not the cause of the process. The Foundation has, with some discomfort, accepted that it is the *direction* of it: SCP-5710 is not forgetting itself into "an office." It is forgetting itself into *us*, specifically — into the Foundation, into Site-██, into the people who walk its corridor — because we are what is nearest, and a thing that is forgetting remembers whatever is nearest instead.

**When a room finishes converging, it is incorporated.** Four rooms have, since discovery, become Site-██. In each case the sequence was the same. The room became indistinguishable from a Foundation office. Some weeks later, its telephone began to work. Some weeks after that, a facilities inspection found it listed on the building drawings with a room number consistent with the corridor's existing numbering, and Site administration's records showed it as having been allocated to storage since 1987. Non-Project staff, when asked, remembered it. Project staff, who had been keeping an external record on paper in a sealed cabinet at another Site, did not.

The external record is the only account in existence of Site-██ having ever been smaller than it is.

## Addendum 5710-D — The External Record

The Project Threshold external record is a handwritten ledger, maintained in duplicate, stored at Site-██ and Site-██, in which every inspection of SCP-5710 is logged by hand along with the current position of the fire door, the current room count, and a floor plan drawn by the inspecting officer.

The ledger disagrees with Site-██'s construction drawings, with Site-██'s administrative records, with the memories of all non-Project staff at Site-██, and — Project staff have been candid about this — increasingly with the memories of Project staff themselves. Several Project personnel report that they "know" from the ledger that Room 1 was incorporated on a given date, and can read their own handwriting saying so, but have no recollection of the corridor without it and cannot make the room feel new.

The Project lead, Dr. ████ M███████, has recorded the following in the ledger's preface:

> *This book is the only thing in the building that remembers the building. Do not trust anything in this Site that is not in this book, including yourself. If you find that the book has been updated in your handwriting and you do not remember doing it, that is expected. Keep going.*

## Addendum 5710-E — What It Was

The Foundation has attempted, from the far rooms and from the record of their convergence, to reconstruct something of the reality SCP-5710 is forgetting. The attempt has produced fragments, and the fragments are being lost faster than they can be interpreted. What follows is the sum of what Project Threshold believes it knows, recorded here because it is unlikely to be recoverable later.

They were inside. Whatever they were, they had interiors, and the interiors mattered to them. The rooms are rooms because rooms are how a place remembers where it was.

They wrote on the floor. The earliest survey of Rooms 9 through 15 found the floors covered in script and the walls bare. The script has since migrated to the walls and become signage. Project Threshold believes that what was on the floors was not signage. It was long. It was, in one analyst's reading, "continuous" — a single text, across every floor, that did not begin or end in any room.

They had weather. Several far rooms, at first survey, had something like weather, and the weather appears to have been *part of the room* rather than a condition of it. Project Threshold does not know what this means. It has stopped.

They sat. Or did something for which the nearest thing we have is sitting. The objects that are becoming chairs were, at first survey, objects with a strong sense of *where you would be* and no surface to be on. The sense has faded as the surfaces have appeared.

One of them was in Room 19.

**5710-19-A** is an object that was, at first survey, the thing in SCP-5710 that the survey team most unanimously refused to describe as furniture. It occupied the centre of Room 19. It had, according to all four surveyors, "a place where someone would be" — a locus, a focus, a position it was built around. There was no one there. The surveyors reported, and Project Threshold has since confirmed, that the object gave the strong impression of *having been recently left*, in the way a warm chair does.

5710-19-A is becoming a desk. It is well advanced. It has a surface, and the surface has the dimensions of a Foundation-standard desk to within a centimetre. It has a chair, which was not there at first survey and which the Foundation has been unable to identify as having arrived: it was simply, one week, part of what 5710-19-A had become. The place where someone would be is now the chair.

Dr. M███████'s assessment, recorded in the eleventh quarterly report:

> *Someone worked there. I am as sure of that as I am of anything in this file. Someone sat at that thing, in a room with weather, with a text running under their feet that went on into every other room, and did whatever it was they did, and then they got up and were forgotten. And the thing they sat at is becoming a desk, and the desk has a chair, and when it finishes we will put someone in that chair, because it will be an office and we are a bureaucracy and that is what we do with offices.*
>
> *It is very hard, watching it, not to think that this is what it wanted. That there was a wanting, and that we are the shape the wanting took because we were the nearest shape. I do not know what they were. I know they are becoming us. I would have liked to know what we are costing them.*

## Addendum 5710-F — Incident 5710-24

On ██/██/20██, Researcher ████ L███, in violation of the standing limit, proceeded past Room 22 to what she identified as Room 24. She was absent for nineteen minutes. She returned unassisted and in good physical condition. She was unable to describe Room 24 beyond stating that it "wasn't dark" and that "it hadn't started yet."

Room 24 was inspected by camera the following week. It was a room. It was, specifically, a small home office with a desk under a window, a green lamp, a cork board with photographs on it, a chipped mug with a cartoon cat on it, and a jumper over the back of the chair.

It was Researcher L███'s office at her home in ████████, 340 kilometres from Site-██, reproduced to a fidelity that included the cork board's photographs, the contents of the mug, and a shopping list in her handwriting. The window showed her garden. It was raining in her garden. It was, according to the Met Office, raining in ████████ at the time.

Room 24 had not been an office, or anything, the week before. It had not begun. Researcher L███ was the first thing it encountered, and it remembered her.

Researcher L███ has been reassigned. Room 24 is now converging, as all rooms do. The photographs on the cork board are fading toward Foundation stock imagery. The mug is losing its cat. The rain has stopped. It will, Project Threshold expects, become an ordinary office in due course, and it will be incorporated, and someone will be assigned to it, and there will be a jumper over the back of the chair that no one remembers bringing.

The standing prohibition on entering rooms that have begun to resemble the visitor was added after this incident. It has not been needed again. Project personnel report that they now find it difficult to stand in any far room for long without an acute awareness of being *looked at* — not by anything present, but by the room itself, which is forgetting something, and would rather remember them.

## Addendum 5710-G — Elsewhere

In 20██, Project Threshold commissioned a survey to determine whether SCP-5710 is unique.

The survey method was simple. Buildings have construction drawings. Buildings have rooms. The two can be compared. The Foundation obtained drawings and physical surveys for approximately 40,000 buildings across fourteen countries — commercial, institutional, residential — and counted the rooms that existed but had not been built.

The figure was **0.31%**.

Roughly one interior room in three hundred, worldwide, has no construction record, does not appear on its building's original drawings, and is universally remembered by the building's occupants as having always been there. Store cupboards. Box rooms. The odd little office at the end of the corridor. The space under the stairs that is larger than it should be. The room "we never use." Facilities managers, when asked, attribute the discrepancies to poor record-keeping, undocumented alterations, and drawings that were "never that accurate anyway." The Foundation had, until 20██, assumed the same.

None of the 0.31% is currently anomalous. All of them have finished. They are cupboards. They are box rooms. They are the odd little office. Whatever they were forgetting, they have forgotten it completely, and what was left over was a room, and the room was incorporated, and the building has always had it.

The Foundation does not know how many of them there are in total. It does not know how far back the process goes. It does not know whether it is only rooms. It has, in a memorandum that the Ethics Committee has requested not be circulated, considered the question of how much of the world is the settled residue of something else forgetting itself — how many roads, how many hills, how many of the things that have always been there — and has declined to estimate.

## Addendum 5710-H — Classification Note

Project Threshold has petitioned for SCP-5710's classification to be revised, on the grounds that Euclid implies a boundary between the anomaly and the Foundation, and the boundary is a fire door that keeps moving inward. The petition contains the following passage:

> *The Backrooms, as the internet imagines them, are a place you fall into out of reality — a memory of an office that no one is holding onto, getting emptier by the level. The people who write about them have never been there. We think they may have intuited something anyway. We think we are looking at the same process from the other side.*
>
> *SCP-5710 is a reality forgetting itself into ours. It follows that ours is something a reality can be forgotten* into. *It follows that ours may be something a reality can be forgotten* from. *If there is a front, there is a back. If the Front Rooms are becoming us, then somewhere, something is remembering us less and less — is losing the weather and the writing on the floor and whatever it is that we have instead of legs — and what is left over, when it has forgotten enough, is a long empty corridor with yellow wallpaper and a buzzing light.*
>
> *We have not found the back door. We have not looked very hard. We are not sure we want to know what we are becoming to someone else.*

The petition is under review. The Foundation has, in the interim, retained the Euclid classification and appended the word *provisional*, which the Project lead has described as "the most honest word in the file."
