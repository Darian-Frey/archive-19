# SCP-6618

**Item #:** SCP-6618

**Object Class:** Neutralised → Reclassified Pending → **Indeterminate**

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-6618 cannot be contained in any conventional sense, as SCP-6618 is not, by any measurable property, anomalous.

The subject of this designation, **PoI-6618** (legal name on file; redacted from this document at the request of the Ethics Committee), continues to reside at her place of residence in ████████, ██████, with no Foundation intervention beyond passive observation. PoI-6618 is unaware of the Foundation's existence. She is unaware of this document's existence. She is unaware that her short fiction has been the subject of seven hundred and forty-two hours of analyst time across three Foundation sites.

Foundation personnel are not to contact PoI-6618 under any circumstances. The Foundation has determined, with high confidence, that contact would not produce useful information and would substantially worsen PoI-6618's quality of life. The Foundation has further determined, with lower but non-trivial confidence, that contact might also worsen the **rate** of the phenomenon. The reasoning is recorded in Addendum 6618-F.

PoI-6618's online activity is monitored continuously via passive collection. New short fiction submissions are flagged on publication and cross-referenced against the active SCP database, the deprecated SCP database, the pending-anomaly registry, and — since ██/██/20██ — the **un-discovered** anomaly projection registry maintained by the Department of Statistical Anomalies.

The cross-referencing returns matches at a rate of approximately 31%. No other author in the monitored writing communities (estimated population: 4.1 million active accounts across twelve platforms) returns a match rate above 0.0003%.

PoI-6618 has not been informed of any of this. PoI-6618 is fine.

---

**Description:** SCP-6618 is the designation given to the phenomenon by which **PoI-6618**, a hobbyist writer of short-form science fiction and horror, produces fiction that corresponds — with high specificity and across multiple independent details — to genuine Foundation-classified anomalies.

PoI-6618 has been writing short fiction as a hobby since approximately 20██. She posts her stories regularly to Reddit (r/nosleep, r/shortscarystories, r/WritingPrompts), to a personal blog hosted on a free platform, and to two smaller writing-community forums. Her stories are typically 500–3,000 words in length. They are well-written. They receive moderate engagement. She has approximately 1,200 followers across her platforms. She does not monetise her writing and has stated, in multiple public comments, that she writes "for fun, when I can't sleep, mostly."

To date, the Foundation has identified **two hundred and eleven** of PoI-6618's published stories that correspond closely to existing Foundation-classified anomalies, recovered objects, or documented incidents. Of these:

- **88** correspond to anomalies whose first detection by Foundation personnel **predates** the story's publication. The Foundation initially classified these as plausible coincidences or as cases where PoI-6618 had encountered a public-domain rumour about the anomaly. Subsequent analysis ruled out both explanations in all 88 cases.
- **94** correspond to anomalies whose first detection by Foundation personnel **postdates** the story's publication, in some cases by years.
- **22** correspond to anomalies whose first detection postdates the story's publication and which were **detected only because Foundation analysts went looking for them** after reading the story.
- **7** correspond to anomalies that, as of the most recent review, **have not been detected**, but which the Department of Statistical Anomalies projects will be detected within the next 5–40 years based on the structural plausibility of the described phenomena.

The seven undiscovered-projection stories have been archived in a sealed file at Site-19. The file is not to be opened except by direct O5 order. The reason is recorded in Addendum 6618-G.

## Addendum 6618-A — Discovery

SCP-6618 was identified on ██/██/20██ by **MTF-Argus-7**, an automated content-monitoring agent deployed by the Foundation across major fiction-hosting platforms. MTF-Argus-7 is, despite the Mobile Task Force designation, not a human team — it is a fine-tuned large language model running continuous semantic comparison between published online fiction and the Foundation's internal anomaly database. Its purpose is to detect leaks: instances where Foundation personnel or contractors have, deliberately or accidentally, published information about real anomalies in fictional form.

Argus-7's typical false-positive rate is approximately one flag per 40,000 stories scanned. The flags are reviewed by human analysts and dismissed in 99.7% of cases as coincidental thematic overlap.

On ██/██/20██, Argus-7 returned a confidence score of **0.94** on a 2,100-word short story titled *"The Garden Door,"* posted to Reddit by user u/██████████████ approximately six hours prior. The story described, in close detail, the visual appearance, behaviour, and containment failure mode of SCP-████, a previously-classified Euclid-class anomaly known to fewer than thirty Foundation personnel.

The human analyst assigned to review the flag, Researcher H████, initially assumed the post indicated a leak. He cleared his afternoon. He pulled the access logs for SCP-████. He cross-referenced the logs against the platform's user-metadata for u/██████████████. The user had been active on Reddit for eight years. She had posted to dozens of subreddits. She had never posted anything that, on review, suggested any connection to the Foundation. She had posted, three months earlier, a long comment in a thread about *Master of Orion II* explaining her preferred fleet composition.

Researcher H████ escalated.

Six weeks later, the Foundation had reviewed all 1,847 stories PoI-6618 had ever published online. The match rate at that initial review was 18%. It has risen, in subsequent reviews, as additional anomalies have been detected that match older stories.

The Foundation has never identified a non-anomalous explanation.

## Addendum 6618-B — Profile of PoI-6618

PoI-6618 is a [REDACTED] year-old [REDACTED] employed as a [REDACTED] in ████████, ██████. She has lived in the same city for [REDACTED] years. She has a small circle of close friends (four, by Foundation count), a partner of [REDACTED] years, and a cat named Crumpet. She visits her parents approximately monthly. She votes in local elections. She has no security clearance with any government or private organisation. She has never travelled outside her home country except on two holidays (one to ████████, one to ████████, both well-documented and unremarkable). She has no degree in physics, mathematics, computer science, or any other field that would predispose her to the speculative content of her fiction. She has, by her own statement, "always just liked weird stuff."

Her interests, based on her public online activity, include:

- Science fiction novels (notably the works of Ursula K. Le Guin, Ted Chiang, Octavia Butler, and Greg Egan)
- Horror short fiction (notably Shirley Jackson, Thomas Ligotti, and the works of various creepypasta authors)
- Retro computing, specifically the **Atari ST** and **Amiga** platforms, on which she primarily plays games released between 1987 and 1995
- Cooking (vegetarian; she has posted approximately forty recipes to her personal blog)
- Cross-stitch
- Two long-running tabletop roleplaying campaigns with her friend group

She has, across her public profiles, never mentioned anything that the Foundation regards as a potential anomaly-adjacent interest. She does not read theoretical physics. She does not follow AI research. She has never posted in any forum dedicated to the paranormal, the occult, conspiracy theory, or unexplained phenomena. Her engagement with the horror genre is, by the Foundation's assessment, entirely literary — she discusses craft, prose style, and emotional resonance, not subject matter. She does not, as far as the Foundation has been able to determine, believe in anything anomalous at all.

Foundation analysts have, with significant reluctance, characterised PoI-6618 as **"the most thoroughly ordinary person currently under Foundation observation."** This characterisation is not facetious. It is the result of a structured profile comparison against a population of approximately 12,000 randomly selected individuals from the same demographic, conducted by the Department of Statistical Anomalies. PoI-6618's profile is more *typical* of her demographic than 99.3% of the comparison population. She is, statistically, **more normal than normal.**

This finding has been a source of some discomfort among the analysts assigned to her case. Researcher H████, in his quarterly report dated ██/██/20██, wrote:

> *We have, by every measure available to us, established that there is nothing unusual about PoI-6618. She is the platonic ideal of a person about whom nothing should be said. The most interesting thing about her, from a profiling standpoint, is that there is nothing interesting about her.*
>
> *This is, of course, the problem. The phenomenon does not have a cause we can identify because there is nothing in her life into which a cause could be embedded. She has no anomalous bloodline, no anomalous diet, no anomalous medical history, no anomalous social contacts, no anomalous hobbies, no anomalous trauma, no anomalous beliefs. She is a woman who likes Le Guin and cross-stitch and an old computer game about space empires. She writes short stories on her lunch break. She is, in every measurable respect, fine.*
>
> *Twenty-two of the anomalies on the active Keter list exist on the active Keter list because she wrote about them first.*

## Addendum 6618-C — Interview Methodology and Findings

PoI-6618 has been interviewed, indirectly, on fourteen occasions. The interviews have been conducted by Foundation personnel posing as ordinary commenters on her stories, asking the kinds of questions that ordinary readers of her stories ask. The questions are tailored to elicit information about her creative process without raising suspicion. The interviews have been productive in the sense that they have generated data. They have been unproductive in the sense that they have not generated *answers.*

A representative selection of PoI-6618's responses, taken from her own public comments and direct replies to Foundation-controlled questioners, is reproduced below.

> **Reader (Foundation):** Where did you get the idea for "The Garden Door"? It's so specific, it almost feels like you saw it somewhere.
>
> **PoI-6618:** Lol thanks! I had a weird dream a couple weeks ago, woke up at like 3am, wrote the whole thing in one sitting before I forgot it. That's how most of these come about honestly. I'm a really vivid dreamer.

> **Reader (Foundation):** Do you ever do research for these? Some of the technical stuff feels really grounded.
>
> **PoI-6618:** Nope! 100% vibes. I just write what feels right. If something needs to sound technical I just kind of make up something that sounds plausible. Sometimes I'll look up a word to make sure I'm using it right but that's about it.

> **Reader (Foundation):** Have you ever felt like one of your stories was, like, real? Like you didn't make it up?
>
> **PoI-6618:** Hahaha okay this is going to sound weird but no, never. I always know I made them up because I remember making them up. Why, do mine feel real to you? That's actually a really nice compliment, thank you.

> **Reader (Foundation):** Have you considered submitting to magazines? Some of these are publishable.
>
> **PoI-6618:** I've thought about it but honestly the submission process sounds like a nightmare and I just like writing them and putting them up here. I have a real job. This is just for fun.

The Foundation's psycholinguistic analysis of PoI-6618's interview responses, applied across the full corpus of her public writing (an estimated 2.8 million words), has detected **no markers** of dishonesty, dissociation, confabulation, foreign cultural patterning, anomalous semantic drift, or covert communication. Her writing voice is consistent across years. Her stated values and personality match her demonstrated behaviour. Her vocabulary is large but unremarkable for a habitual reader of literary science fiction. She uses the Oxford comma. She is mildly opinionated about *Star Trek*. She thinks pineapple on pizza is fine. She is, in every available metric, **telling the truth.**

She does not know.

The Foundation has determined, after seven months of analysis, that PoI-6618 is not concealing the source of her ideas. There is no source to conceal. She is, exactly as she has stated, dreaming the dreams of an ordinary person and writing down what she remembers. The dreams happen to contain Keter-class anomalies in approximately one out of every three cases. The dreams do not, by any neurological or parapsychological test the Foundation has been able to administer remotely, differ from ordinary dreams in any detectable respect.

She just dreams. The dreams are correct.

## Addendum 6618-D — The Absence of Anomaly

The Department of Anomalous Phenomena has conducted, at the request of the SCP-6618 case team, an exhaustive remote screening of PoI-6618 for every known transmission vector, infection pattern, contact signature, or environmental anomaly that could plausibly account for the phenomenon. The screening included:

- **Memetic analysis.** PoI-6618's writing has been screened against all 1,400+ known memetic agents in the Foundation's database. No matches. Her writing has *no embedded patterns* — not even the trace signatures that all human writing carries from cultural memetic transmission. She is unusually clean.
- **Cognitohazard exposure history.** No detected exposure events. Her browsing history (passively collected) contains no cognitohazardous material. She has not, by available records, ever been in proximity to any classified cognitohazardous object.
- **Reality-bending screening.** PoI-6618 has been classified Type-Green-Null. She does not bend reality. Reality is not bent around her. She does not appear in any reality-bender's "trail" that the Foundation has been able to reconstruct.
- **Precognitive screening.** PoI-6618 displays no markers of precognitive sensitivity. Standard remote precognitive tests (administered via subtle stimuli embedded in her social media feed) produce results indistinguishable from chance. She is not a precog. She does not see the future. The Foundation has verified this against thirty-one independent statistical baselines.
- **Narrative-bleed screening.** PoI-6618 does not appear to be a "ascended character" or narrative-layer migrant (see *Narrativistics and You*, Foundation reference document). She has a complete personal history, verifiable to her birth certificate and beyond, with no narrative-causal discontinuities.
- **Entity-contact screening.** No detected entities. No detected proximities to entities. Her dreams, sampled via the imperfect proxy of her own public descriptions of them, do not feature recurring presences, voices, or instructive figures.
- **Bloodline screening.** PoI-6618's family history, to the extent it could be traced, contains no anomalous individuals. Her grandfather served in the [REDACTED] during [REDACTED] but never in any capacity that would have brought him into anomaly-adjacent contact. Her great-aunt, on her mother's side, was an amateur astronomer who published one paper on variable stars in 1962. The paper is non-anomalous.
- **Environmental screening.** Her residence has been remotely surveyed for thaumic emissions, Hume gradients, anomalous radiation, and atypical EM signatures. All readings are within normal civilian ranges. Her cat is also non-anomalous.

The Foundation has, at this point, screened PoI-6618 against every known mechanism by which a non-anomalous person could acquire anomalous information. **All screenings have returned negative.**

The Foundation has not concluded that the screenings are inadequate. The screenings are the same screenings that have, in 9,341 prior cases, correctly identified the mechanism by which a person became a vector or witness for an anomaly.

The Foundation has, with great reluctance, concluded that **the mechanism in PoI-6618's case is not on the list of known mechanisms.** And that the absence of any detectable anomaly is itself the only available datum.

She is normal. She is correct. The two facts are simultaneous.

## Addendum 6618-E — Selected Correspondences

The Foundation regards the full list of 211 confirmed correspondences as classified at Level 5 and restricted to the SCP-6618 case team. A small selection, redacted of specific anomaly designations and details, is reproduced below to illustrate the range of the phenomenon.

**Story:** *"The Long Lunch"* (published ██/██/20██). A short story (1,400 words) about a man who realises, gradually over the course of a normal workday, that his colleagues are not the same people they were that morning, and that he himself may not be the same person he was. The story ends with the man eating lunch and not being able to remember what he ordered.

**Correspondence:** SCP-████, an anomaly first contained ██/██/20██ (**eleven months after publication**), which produces effects substantially similar to the story, including the specific symptom of inability to recall recent food preferences. Foundation discovery of the anomaly was via routine sweep; investigators were not aware of PoI-6618's story until Argus-7 retrospectively flagged it three years later.

---

**Story:** *"A Better Map"* (published ██/██/20██). A short story (2,800 words) about a cartographer who discovers that the official maps of his country are subtly wrong — not in obvious ways, but in ways that, when corrected, allow travellers to reach destinations approximately 3% faster. The cartographer becomes obsessed with producing the "true map" and gradually loses the ability to navigate his own home.

**Correspondence:** SCP-████, a class of cartographic anomalies whose existence was, prior to PoI-6618's story, considered to be unsubstantiated rumour within the Foundation. Following the story's publication, a junior researcher who had read it (recreationally; she was not aware of PoI-6618 at the time) proposed re-investigating the rumours. The anomalies were confirmed to exist within six weeks.

---

**Story:** *"Crumpet Goes Out"* (published ██/██/20██). A short story (700 words) about the author's cat going missing for an evening and returning home with no memory of where she had been, but with a small wooden token in her collar that the author cannot identify.

**Correspondence:** [REDACTED]. The case team's recommendation is that this correspondence remain classified at the highest available level. The cat in the story is named after PoI-6618's real cat. The wooden token described in the story is a precise match for a class of objects the Foundation has been tracking for eighteen years. **PoI-6618 has never been in a position to encounter one of these objects.** PoI-6618's cat is, by Foundation veterinary surveillance, healthy and non-anomalous, and has never been observed to leave the apartment unsupervised.

---

**Story:** *"The Most Boring Job in the World"* (published ██/██/20██). A short story (1,100 words) about a person who works for a secret organisation whose entire function is to *not notice things.* The narrator describes her workday as consisting primarily of carefully averting her attention from objects, places, and people that her organisation has designated as "below the threshold of acknowledgement." The story ends with the narrator wondering whether she herself has been designated as below the threshold by someone else, somewhere, and what it would mean if she had.

**Correspondence:** No active anomaly matches this story. The Department of Statistical Anomalies has flagged it as one of the seven "projection" cases. The flag's notes read, in full: *"This is structurally plausible. We cannot rule out that it describes something we have been institutionally configured not to see. We recommend that this story not be re-read by personnel cleared for SCP-6618 unless operationally necessary."*

## Addendum 6618-F — The Non-Contact Recommendation

The Ethics Committee's formal recommendation, issued ██/██/20██, that PoI-6618 not be contacted under any circumstances is reproduced in part below.

> *We have considered, in detail, the operational arguments for direct contact with PoI-6618. They are not without weight. We could ask her, directly, what she is doing. We could observe her under controlled conditions. We could place her under protective custody, ostensibly for her safety, and gain access to a level of behavioural data the passive collection cannot provide.*
>
> *We have rejected all of these options. The reasoning is as follows.*
>
> *PoI-6618 is, by every available metric, a person living an ordinary, contented life. She has friends. She has a partner she loves. She has a cat. She has a job she does not hate. She writes stories on her lunch break and posts them online for fun. She is, by the Foundation's own analytical standards, the most psychologically healthy individual currently in any of our databases. We do not have a single other person under observation about whom this is true.*
>
> *Contact would change this. We are not able to predict how — but we are able to predict, with very high confidence, that any contact framing we could devise would damage her in some way. If we tell her the truth, she will be terrified, and she will be terrified for the rest of her life. If we tell her a cover story, she will know we are lying, because her own writing has, on three separate occasions, accurately described Foundation cover-story protocols. We cannot lie to her. We cannot tell her the truth. We can only leave her alone.*
>
> *There is a second consideration, which we have debated extensively. The phenomenon — whatever it is — appears to be a function of PoI-6618's ordinary, undisturbed life. We do not know what would happen to the phenomenon if her life ceased to be ordinary. We do not know whether contact would make the phenomenon stop, intensify, or change form. We have no way to know in advance.*
>
> *Given that the phenomenon, as currently configured, is producing for the Foundation a steady stream of high-quality intelligence about emerging anomalies — intelligence we would not otherwise have, intelligence that has already enabled the preemptive containment of at least four Keter-class threats — we have determined that the operational case for contact is, in fact, negative. PoI-6618 is, in her current configuration, the most valuable intelligence asset the Foundation possesses. She does not know this. She must not know this. The asset is the unawareness.*
>
> *We are recommending that no Foundation personnel contact her, ever, for any reason. We are recommending that her existence be classified at the highest available level. We are recommending that, in the event of her death (which the Department of Actuarial Anomalies projects will occur of natural causes in approximately forty-three years), all collected materials related to her be sealed and reviewed only after a one-hundred-year cooling-off period.*
>
> *We are recommending, in short, that the Foundation do the one thing it is least institutionally suited to doing: nothing. We are recommending that we let her live her life.*
>
> *We acknowledge that this recommendation is unusual. We acknowledge that it conflicts with the Foundation's standard operational doctrine. We submit it anyway. We believe it is correct.*
>
> *We also acknowledge that we are uncertain — and that this uncertainty is itself diagnostic — whether the recommendation is correct because it is genuinely ethically and operationally optimal, or because PoI-6618 has, somewhere in her two-point-eight million published words, already written a story about a Foundation Ethics Committee that decided not to contact a normal woman with a cat, and that story shaped our thinking before any of us read it.*
>
> *We have searched her published corpus. We have not found such a story.*
>
> *We acknowledge that this does not necessarily mean she has not written it.*

The recommendation was accepted by O5 vote, 11-2. The two dissenting votes are not recorded.

## Addendum 6618-G — The Seven Sealed Stories

The seven stories identified by the Department of Statistical Anomalies as "projection" cases — stories that correspond to no currently-detected anomaly but which describe phenomena considered structurally plausible — have been archived in a sealed file at Site-19. The file is locked behind a quad-witness protocol and is not to be opened except by direct O5 order.

The reason for the sealing, recorded in the file's cover memo, is as follows:

> *Reading these stories appears to constitute a mechanism for detecting the anomalies they describe. The junior researcher who first proposed re-investigating the cartographic rumours after reading "A Better Map" did so because the story had made her aware that the rumours were testable. She was not, prior to reading, a cartographic specialist. She had no reason to be looking for that anomaly. She found it because PoI-6618's story put her in a frame of mind in which finding it became possible.*
>
> *We have, on review, determined that this mechanism is the only consistent explanation for the 22 cases in which Foundation personnel detected an anomaly after reading PoI-6618's story about it. The stories do not cause the anomalies. The stories cause the detection. The anomalies were always there.*
>
> *The seven sealed stories therefore describe seven anomalies that are, with high probability, currently extant in the world and undetected. Reading the stories would, with non-trivial probability, cause them to be detected. We have determined that the Foundation is not, at present, prepared to detect them. We do not know what they are. We do not know how serious they are. We do not know whether the Foundation's current capacity is adequate to contain them.*
>
> *We have therefore elected, against considerable internal disagreement, to leave them in the dark. PoI-6618 has, by the act of writing them, given us a choice we did not previously have: the choice of whether to know. We have, for the moment, chosen not to know. We acknowledge that this choice is reversible, and may become reversible under conditions of duress (a crisis sufficiently severe that we conclude the projected anomalies could not be worse than the present situation). We have built the seal accordingly.*
>
> *We note, finally, that PoI-6618 has continued to write. Approximately every six weeks, she publishes a new story. The Foundation reviews each new story on publication. Some are non-anomalous. Some correspond to known anomalies. Some correspond to anomalies not yet detected. Some, occasionally, are added to the sealed file.*
>
> *The sealed file currently contains seven stories. It is expected to contain more.*

## Addendum 6618-H — Researcher H████'s Personal Note

Researcher H████, the original analyst assigned to SCP-6618, was reassigned to other duties on ██/██/20██ at his own request, after approximately three years on the case. He submitted, upon reassignment, a brief personal note for the case file. The note is included here, with his permission.

> *I want to say something for the record that does not belong in any of the formal reports.*
>
> *In the three years I have been reading PoI-6618's stories, I have read approximately 2.4 million of her words. They are good stories. They are not great stories — she will, I suspect, never be published, because she does not want to be, and because what she writes is not quite what the magazines are looking for. But they are good. They are warm. They have a sense of humour. The protagonists are usually women who have ordinary problems and who solve them through patience, attention, and small acts of kindness toward the people around them. The horror, when it comes, is almost always the horror of a small ordinary thing being disturbed.*
>
> *I do not know how she does what she does. I do not know whether she knows. I have read every word she has written and I have come to feel, in a way I cannot defend professionally, that I know her, slightly, and that I like her. She would, I think, be a good person to have as a neighbour. She would not be a good person to have as an enemy, but only because she would be devastated by the conflict, not because she would do anything I could not endure.*
>
> *I am leaving this assignment because I have started to worry that I have become, in some sense, one of her readers, and that the Foundation does not benefit from analysts who are too well-acquainted with the work of the subject. My replacement will not have this problem, at first. I suspect they will, eventually. I do not have a recommendation for this.*
>
> *I want to note, finally, that the Ethics Committee's recommendation that we leave her alone is, in my opinion, the correct recommendation. Not for the operational reasons they listed, which are real and which I agree with. For a simpler reason. She is happy. She does not need us. Whatever is happening to her — whatever she is — she has built a life around it that is good, and that is hers, and that does not include us.*
>
> *We have spent three years and seven hundred and forty-two analyst-hours on the question of what she is. I do not have an answer. But I want to suggest, for the record, that "what she is" may not be the right question. She is a woman who writes stories. The stories happen to be correct. There is no second layer underneath that. The Foundation is not used to anomalies without second layers. I am not sure we have ever encountered one before.*
>
> *I hope, very much, that we leave her alone.*

**Status:** Indeterminate. Observation continues. Subject undisturbed.
**Outstanding Question:** None. She is fine.

We hope she stays that way.
