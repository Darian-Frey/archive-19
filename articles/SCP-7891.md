# SCP-7891

**Item #:** SCP-7891

**Object Class:** Safe

**Special Containment Procedures:** The original instance of SCP-7891 is located within the bounds of ████████ Green, a small public park of approximately 0.7 hectares situated at the centre of the hamlet of ████████, in the London Borough of ████████████. The park, the surrounding hamlet, and SCP-7891 itself remain accessible to the general public.

The Foundation maintains a passive observation post in a converted ground-floor flat overlooking the park, staffed by two embedded agents posing as the property's tenants — a postdoctoral researcher and her partner. Their cover work (the former at the nearby ████████ University, the latter as a freelance illustrator) is genuine. They have been in place since 2017. Both have requested, and been granted, indefinite postings.

Cultivation of SCP-7891 for managed therapeutic use is conducted under **Project Saplings**, a long-term programme administered jointly by the Foundation's Department of Biological Anomalies and the Department of Anomalous Public Health. The programme is described in Addendum 7891-E.

The original ████████ Green site is not to be disturbed, harvested from, or subjected to invasive sampling beyond the carefully circumscribed protocols established in 2018. The site has been determined to be the *source population* of SCP-7891, and the Foundation has made an institutional commitment, recorded in Addendum 7891-F, that the source population's welfare takes precedence over any operational consideration.

The hamlet's residents are not aware of SCP-7891's specific nature. They are aware that their park is unusually pleasant. They are content with this awareness, and the Foundation has determined that no further disclosure is necessary or beneficial.

---

**Description:** SCP-7891 is a species of mycorrhizal fungus, currently undescribed in the mycological literature, exhibiting properties not observed in any other known fungus.

The fungus's primary biological function is unremarkable in form, if not in efficiency: SCP-7891 forms symbiotic associations with the root systems of vascular plants, exchanging nutrients (predominantly phosphorus, nitrogen, and trace minerals) for carbohydrates produced by host plant photosynthesis. This is the standard mycorrhizal relationship documented in approximately 90% of vascular plant species worldwide.

The anomalous properties of SCP-7891 are twofold.

**First**, the symbiotic exchange is approximately **eight to twelve times more efficient** than the equivalent relationship in known mycorrhizal species. Plants associated with SCP-7891 receive substantially greater nutrient uptake from soil resources, and grow with markedly enhanced vigour, colour saturation, and longevity. The visible result, at the ████████ Green site, is a park whose grass remains lush throughout the year, whose flowerbeds display chromatic intensity exceeding any horticultural baseline, and whose mature trees show growth-ring patterns suggesting accelerated but otherwise healthy development. The site is, by every available aesthetic and ecological measure, **exceptionally beautiful**, and has been informally known by this characterisation among local residents since at least the 1970s.

**Second**, and the property for which SCP-7891 was designated as anomalous, the fungus releases reproductive spores into the air at densities approximately three to five times higher than those of comparable wild mycorrhizal species. The spores are too small and dilute to be visible. The spores are, in standard mycological terms, **harmless**: they are non-pathogenic, do not establish colonies in human tissue, do not provoke allergenic or inflammatory responses, and are filtered or expelled from the respiratory tract within hours of inhalation without complication.

The spores, when inhaled by humans at the ambient densities present in the vicinity of ████████ Green, produce a **measurable, sustained improvement in the inhaler's mental health**. This improvement has been characterised by the Foundation across eight years of investigation. It is reproduced in summary form below.

The effect manifests as:

- A statistically significant reduction in subjective symptoms of depression, anxiety, and rumination.
- A measurable elevation in mood, attentional focus, and sense of subjective wellbeing.
- Improved sleep quality, particularly reduced time-to-sleep-onset.
- Reduced biomarker indicators of chronic stress (cortisol, inflammatory cytokines).
- A small but reliable increase in social engagement and self-reported sense of connection.

The effect onset is gradual, becoming subjectively noticeable after approximately three to five visits to the affected area (each visit of 20+ minutes' duration). The effect is dose-dependent, in that more frequent or longer visits produce more pronounced and durable effects, up to a plateau that appears to correspond to clinical "remission-equivalent" levels for mild-to-moderate depressive and anxiety disorders. The effect is reversible: cessation of exposure produces a slow decline back to baseline over approximately four to eight weeks.

**No adverse effects have been identified** in eight years of investigation, encompassing biomarker monitoring, neurological screening, psychological assessment, and detailed health surveillance of over 1,200 residents and regular visitors to the site.

The single behavioural effect that the Foundation has flagged for ongoing monitoring is a measurable increase in the time exposed individuals spend in green spaces and parks generally, including parks unconnected to SCP-7891. Affected individuals report enjoying parks more, feeling more drawn to walk through them, and choosing routes that include green space when alternatives are available. The Foundation has, after extensive review, classified this as **a secondary benefit rather than a side effect**, on the grounds that increased time in green space is itself a well-documented contributor to mental and physical health, and the affected individuals are demonstrably happier as a result.

The Department of Anomalous Public Health has, in an unusual departure from standard reporting conventions, recorded this finding in its official assessment with the following sentence:

> *The principal long-term effect of SCP-7891 on exposed humans is that they want to go for more walks. We have, after extensive deliberation, determined that this is fine.*

## Addendum 7891-A — Discovery

SCP-7891 came to Foundation attention through an entirely conventional public health channel. In 2016, a graduate student at ████████ University, conducting a thesis project on the geographic distribution of mental health diagnoses across Greater London, identified a small anomalous cluster: a hamlet of approximately 380 residents in the borough of ████████████ whose recorded rates of depression, anxiety disorder, and stress-related illness were **approximately 40% below the London regional average**.

The student initially attributed the cluster to small-sample noise, demographic factors, or self-selection effects (the hamlet is generally considered desirable, with property prices reflecting this). She controlled for these variables and the anomaly persisted. She submitted the finding as an unremarked statistical curiosity in her thesis, which was awarded a distinction.

Her thesis supervisor, a clinical psychiatrist with a side interest in environmental psychiatry, found the finding more compelling and submitted a brief discussion to a regional public health newsletter. The newsletter is monitored by a Foundation embedded asset at the NHS London region office, who flagged the discussion for review under standing instructions to identify possible Foundation-relevant patterns in public health data.

A preliminary investigation was authorised in late 2016. A field team conducted what was, at the time, expected to be a brief verification visit. They identified the heat-map centre of the mental health effect within forty-eight hours. The centre was the park.

The fungus was identified, by structural analysis of soil samples collected non-invasively from the park's perimeter, six weeks later. Its anomalous spore production and behavioural effect on humans were established by the end of 2017.

The graduate student and her supervisor have not been informed of any of this. Their thesis remains in the university library, where it has been borrowed three times. The Foundation has, with quiet appreciation, ensured that the student has received a small unsolicited research grant from a fictitious foundation each year since her graduation. She has used the grants to support her postdoctoral work in environmental psychiatry, which she describes as her life's vocation. The Foundation regards this as an investment.

## Addendum 7891-B — The Heat Map

The initial spatial mapping of SCP-7891's effect, conducted in early 2017, produced one of the most aesthetically pleasing documents in the Department of Biological Anomalies' archive. The map plots aggregate self-reported wellbeing scores for hamlet residents and regular visitors against geographic distance from the park's centre. The scores form a near-perfect radial gradient: highest at the park, declining smoothly with distance, returning to regional baseline at approximately 600 metres from the park's edge.

The map is informally known within the Department as **"the bullseye."** It has been reproduced in framed copies in the offices of three Department directors. The current Director, Dr. ████████, keeps her copy on the wall behind her desk, where visitors can see it. When asked about it, she typically replies: *"That's an SCP. That's one of the ones we got right."*

The visualisation has, internally, become a touchstone for the Department's institutional culture. New researchers joining the Department of Biological Anomalies are typically shown the bullseye in their first week, with the explanation that the Department's mission is not exclusively containment — that sometimes, very rarely, the Department encounters an anomaly that the world should be allowed to keep.

## Addendum 7891-C — The Hamlet

████████ is a small hamlet of approximately 380 residents in the London Borough of ████████████. Its physical character is, by London standards, atypical: a cluster of late-Victorian and Edwardian terraced houses arranged around a Georgian-era green, with a single pub (the ████████ Arms), a small parish church (St. ████████'s), a corner shop, and a primary school of 78 pupils. The hamlet is bordered on three sides by main roads and on the fourth by a small wood that buffers it from the adjacent A-road. It is not on the tourist map. Most Londoners do not know it exists.

The Foundation has conducted, with appropriate discretion, an extensive characterisation of the hamlet's social and cultural life. The findings can be summarised as follows: it is a *very nice place to live.*

Residents have, by Foundation survey, the following characteristics relative to regional baselines:

- Higher than average reported life satisfaction
- Higher than average rate of long-term residency (median tenure: 17 years, versus 5.4 years for the borough)
- Higher than average rate of multigenerational households (24% versus 8%)
- Lower than average rate of divorce, family conflict, and acute family-services intervention
- Higher than average rate of participation in community activities (parish, pub, school, local volunteer organisations)
- Higher than average rate of casual social contact (neighbours speaking, children playing together, informal gatherings on the green)
- A lower than expected rate of crime, vandalism, and antisocial behaviour, even controlling for socioeconomic factors

These characteristics are partially attributable to SCP-7891 and partially attributable to the kind of community that develops in a place where people are happy and stay for a long time. The Foundation has not been able to cleanly separate the two contributions, and has not seen any operational reason to try.

The hamlet has, in the years since SCP-7891 was identified, become a site of quiet ongoing study. Field researchers who have spent time in ████████ have, with consistency, reported finding the experience of being there subtly memorable. Several have requested longer assignments. Two have, with appropriate Foundation oversight, relocated to ████████ permanently after their retirement. They are aware of SCP-7891. They report that they would have wanted to move there even if they had not been.

## Addendum 7891-D — Mechanism

SCP-7891's antidepressant action has been characterised, to the extent the Foundation has been able to do so, through controlled in vitro and in vivo studies conducted at Site-43 Biological Annex. The mechanism is not fully understood. What is known is described below.

The spores contain a class of small-molecule compounds, designated **7891-A through 7891-G**, with structural similarity to known serotonergic neuromodulators but with novel pharmacodynamic properties. The compounds appear to act on multiple neuroreceptor classes simultaneously, with a profile most closely resembling a combined SSRI, anxiolytic, and mild neurotrophic agent — but with none of the side-effect liabilities associated with any of those drug classes individually.

Crucially, the compounds are present in the spores at extremely low concentrations and are released into the inhaler's bloodstream at sub-therapeutic doses by conventional pharmacological standards. The doses are, by every available calculation, too small to produce the observed effects.

The Foundation's current working hypothesis is that the compounds act as *signalling molecules* rather than as direct pharmacological agents — that is, they do not bind to receptors at sufficient density to produce the observed effects directly, but instead **trigger endogenous mood-regulatory processes in the inhaler's own neurochemistry**, which then produce the effects at therapeutic magnitudes. SCP-7891 is, on this model, less a drug than a *prompt*: it gives the brain a small, very specific instruction, and the brain does the rest of the work itself.

This mechanism, if correct, has significant implications. The fungus is not medicating people. It is, in effect, asking their brains to behave the way healthy brains behave, and the brains are obliging. The absence of side effects is consistent with this — the brain does not produce undesirable side effects when its own homeostatic processes are properly engaged.

The Foundation has, with significant deliberation, characterised this mechanism as **"a fungus that politely reminds the brain to be well."** This characterisation is not formal. It is, however, the one most commonly used in internal discussions, and has appeared in three Departmental memoranda.

## Addendum 7891-E — Project Saplings

Project Saplings is the Foundation's long-term cultivation programme for SCP-7891. The programme was initiated in 2019, following completion of the initial characterisation work and a unanimous Ethics Committee finding that the Foundation had both the capacity and the moral obligation to attempt responsible cultivation.

The programme's stated objectives are:

1. To cultivate SCP-7891 outside its original site without harming the source population.
2. To develop standardised protocols for therapeutic application, including dose, duration, and delivery method.
3. To make the resulting therapy available, in stages and under appropriate oversight, to populations with the greatest need.
4. To do so without revealing the existence of SCP-7891 to the general public or to the wider medical establishment, while still ensuring that the therapy reaches the people it is intended to help.

Objective 4 has, predictably, proven the most operationally challenging. The Foundation has been required to design distribution mechanisms that allow SCP-7891 to be deployed under the cover of conventional public health initiatives. The current operational solution involves the deliberate establishment of new green spaces — small parks, churchyards, community gardens — in locations selected for proximity to populations with high mental health need (areas of socioeconomic deprivation, university residential districts, refugee resettlement neighbourhoods). The green spaces are constructed under cover of ordinary municipal regeneration projects, funded through Foundation-controlled grant programs disguised as charitable initiatives.

SCP-7891 is then introduced to the soil of the new green space, where it establishes itself in symbiosis with the planted vegetation and begins releasing spores. The effect is measurable in the surrounding population within approximately 18 to 24 months.

As of ██/██/20██, Project Saplings has established **forty-seven** secondary SCP-7891 sites across the United Kingdom, with additional sites in Germany, France, the Netherlands, Canada, and Australia. The aggregate population estimated to benefit from these sites is approximately **210,000 people**, with the figure growing as the spore distributions expand and new sites are established.

The Foundation's projection, based on current expansion rates and the absence of significant obstacles, is that SCP-7891 will be measurably affecting the mental health of approximately **two million people** by 2040 and approximately **fifteen million** by 2060. The Foundation has, with characteristic understatement, recorded these projections as "encouraging."

The programme has, since its initiation, identified **no adverse effects** in any of the recipient populations. The expansion is proceeding ahead of schedule. The Department of Anomalous Public Health has, on three occasions, requested expanded budget allocations to accelerate the programme. The requests have been granted on all three occasions, with unusually little debate.

## Addendum 7891-F — The ████████ Green Commitment

The Foundation has, in the formal documentation of SCP-7891, recorded a commitment that has no precedent in its institutional history.

The commitment, drafted by the Ethics Committee in 2018 and ratified by O5 vote, reads in full:

> *The original source population of SCP-7891, located at ████████ Green in the hamlet of ████████, London, will be preserved and protected in perpetuity. No invasive sampling, harvesting, transplantation, or experimental intervention will be conducted at the original site beyond the minimum necessary to maintain the cultivation programme at secondary sites. The park will remain a public space. The residents of the hamlet will continue to enjoy the benefits of the original site as they have for at least the past fifty years and, by all available evidence, for substantially longer.*
>
> *The Foundation acknowledges that SCP-7891 is, in some meaningful sense, a gift — to the residents of ████████, to the wider population that will eventually benefit from Project Saplings, and to the Foundation itself, which has been given the opportunity to do something straightforwardly good with its capabilities. The Foundation accepts this gift with gratitude and with the obligation that gifts entail.*
>
> *We will not damage the source. We will not exhaust it. We will not, in our enthusiasm for what it can do for the wider world, forget that it was first a small park in a small hamlet, where ordinary people walked their dogs and met their neighbours and slept slightly better at night, and that this remains its principal purpose.*
>
> *We will protect it. We will share what comes from it. We will tell, eventually, the story of how this came to be — when the world is ready, and when we have done enough good with it that we have earned the telling.*
>
> *We will not, for as long as this institution exists, do anything to harm ████████ Green.*

The commitment has, in the seven years since its ratification, been observed without exception. It is read aloud at the orientation of new personnel assigned to Project Saplings. The Department of Biological Anomalies has, internally, taken to referring to it simply as **the Promise.**

The Promise is regarded, within the relevant Departments, as one of the Foundation's better institutional documents.

## Addendum 7891-G — A Note on Classification

SCP-7891 has, since its identification, prompted ongoing internal discussion about whether the Foundation's standard object classification system is adequate to the phenomenon. The classifications available — Safe, Euclid, Keter, Thaumiel, Apollyon, Neutralised — all describe relationships between the Foundation and the anomaly that are fundamentally adversarial: containment, monitoring, weaponisation, suppression. None describes the relationship the Foundation has actually established with SCP-7891.

The Department of Biological Anomalies submitted, in 2021, a formal proposal that the Foundation adopt an additional classification, provisionally named **Cynicus** (Latin: "of the cynic philosophers," referring to objects that the Foundation observes simply for the sake of its own institutional improvement), to designate anomalies whose principal containment requirement is to **not be ruined** by the Foundation's involvement.

The proposal was, after eighteen months of inter-Departmental debate, rejected. The official reason given was that introducing a new classification for a single phenomenon was not warranted by the operational record. The unofficial reason, recorded in the minutes of the meeting at which the proposal was finally rejected, was as follows:

> *We have decided not to create a new classification for SCP-7891 because we believe doing so would, in some symbolic sense, separate it from the rest of our work. SCP-7891 is contained. It is Safe. It is, by the standards of this institution, doing exactly what we want it to do. We do not need a special category for it. We need the rest of our work to be more like it.*

SCP-7891 is therefore classified Safe, like a self-replicating ceramic vessel or a particularly well-behaved spatial fold. The classification fits the formal criteria. It is, by all accounts, fine.

## Addendum 7891-H — The Garden at Site-43

In early 2022, Dr. ████████, the Director of the Department of Biological Anomalies, submitted an unusual budget request to the Site-43 administrative authority. The request was for the construction of a small landscaped courtyard — approximately 240 square metres, situated in the central atrium of the Biological Annex — to be planted with mixed grasses, perennial flowers, two ornamental trees, and a small water feature, with seating for approximately twenty personnel. The request specified that the soil for the courtyard be supplied from a Project Saplings cultivation site and inoculated with SCP-7891.

The request was approved within forty-eight hours, with a brevity of administrative discussion that the Site-43 records office noted was unusual.

The Garden, as it has come to be known, was completed in late 2022 and has been in continuous use since. It is situated under a glass roof, receives natural light throughout the day, and is accessible to all Site-43 personnel during working hours. There are no monitoring instruments in the Garden. It is, by Dr. ████████'s explicit and repeated insistence, not a laboratory. It is a garden. The fungus is doing what the fungus does. The personnel are doing what personnel do when given access to a garden.

A standing Departmental encouragement, formalised in mid-2023, suggests that Biological Annex staff take regular breaks in the Garden, conduct one-on-one meetings there in preference to conference rooms when weather and confidentiality permit, and consider the space for thinking time when working on difficult problems. The encouragement is not mandatory. Compliance, by Foundation HR survey, is approximately 91% of eligible personnel, which is the highest voluntary participation rate the Site has ever recorded for any wellness initiative.

The secondary effect anticipated by Dr. ████████ has, in the years since, become visible. Site-43 personnel have, increasingly, brought small houseplants into their offices and workstations. The Site's facilities maintenance team reports that, as of the most recent inventory, **approximately 78% of personal workstations at Site-43 have at least one plant**, with the average being two to three. The figures are highest in the Biological Annex (94%) but are now substantial across all departments, including those with no formal connection to SCP-7891 work — the Department of Resilient Cognition, Computational Anomalies, the Site's clerical and security divisions.

The plants are not anomalous. They are ordinary houseplants — pothos, peace lilies, small succulents, the occasional fern. The Foundation has, on three separate occasions, screened a sample of staff plants for inadvertent SCP-7891 colonisation and found none. The plants are doing nothing more than what plants ordinarily do, which is to be quietly green and alive in the corners of rooms.

The effect on Site-43 personnel has, nonetheless, been measurable. Mental health-related absences from work are down 23% across the Site since 2022. Self-reported job satisfaction is up across all departments. Voluntary attrition has dropped to the lowest level in the Site's recorded history. The Department of Resilient Cognition, which has historically had the highest rate of staff burnout in the Foundation, has reported a 38% reduction in burnout-related transfer requests since the Garden was installed.

The Department of Resilient Cognition does not have any direct access to SCP-7891. The Garden is in the Biological Annex, on the opposite side of the Site. Most Resilient Cognition staff visit it once a week at most. The effect on their wellbeing, in the Director's own assessment, **cannot be entirely attributable to SCP-7891 exposure.** Something else is happening. The most plausible explanation, by the Department's own analysis, is that Site-43 personnel are, since the Garden's installation, *behaving differently*. They take more breaks. They walk more. They notice plants. They bring plants to work. They ask their colleagues about their plants. They are, by every available measure, **looking after themselves and each other slightly better**, and the cumulative effect of this slightly-better is showing up across the metrics.

Dr. ████████, when asked about the secondary effect at a Departmental briefing in 2024, offered the following characterisation:

> *"I did not build the Garden to medicate my staff. I built it because I thought my staff might enjoy having a garden, and I was correct. The plants on their desks are not the fungus. The walks they take are not the fungus. The fact that two researchers from Computational Anomalies have started a lunchtime running club is not the fungus. The fungus, as best we can tell, is doing one small thing: it is improving the mood of the people who actually visit the Garden, by a measurable but modest amount. Everything else is what people do when they remember that they like being alive. The Garden, I think, is mostly a reminder."*

The Department of Anomalous Public Health has, in light of the Site-43 data, initiated a pilot programme to install Project Saplings courtyards at three additional Foundation sites. The pilot is in its early phases. Results are anticipated by 2027.

The current operational doctrine of the Department of Biological Anomalies, on this point, is summarised in a single sentence at the top of the Project Saplings field manual:

> *Where you can, plant something. Where you can't, water what is already there.*

The Department has, internally, taken to applying this doctrine quite broadly.

## Addendum 7891-I — Closing Note

The following note was attached, without explanation, to the most recent quarterly report of Project Saplings by the Department's Director, Dr. ████████. It is reproduced here at her request.

> *I walked through ████████ Green last week. I do not, ordinarily, visit the site — the Foundation has agents there for that purpose, and my presence would be operationally unhelpful. But I had occasion to be in the borough on other business, and the park was on my route, and I detoured through it.*
>
> *It was an autumn afternoon. The grass was a particular green that I do not believe I have ever seen in another park in England. There were three children playing on the swings, watched by a man who was reading the paper on the nearest bench. An elderly woman was walking a small dog along the path. The dog was a terrier of some kind, and was extremely interested in a particular patch of leaves. Someone had recently planted bulbs along the southern edge of the green. There was a bench dedicated to a man named Reginald who had loved this park.*
>
> *I sat on the bench for approximately ten minutes. I will not say that the spores affected me, because I do not believe ten minutes is sufficient exposure to produce the documented effect. But I will say, for the record, that I left the park feeling somewhat better than I had felt when I entered it, and that this feeling has, in the days since, persisted.*
>
> *I have, in seventeen years at this Foundation, contained many things. I have written many reports. I have approved many containment protocols. I have, on more occasions than I can count, been the person in the room whose job it was to determine what the world should not be allowed to know about itself.*
>
> *Project Saplings is the work I am proudest of. I am writing this down because I want it to be on the record, in case there is a future Director who needs to be reminded of why we are doing this. We are doing it because we have, for once, found something the world should be allowed to keep, and we have decided to help that be true.*
>
> *I would like, when I retire, to move to ████████. I have already spoken to my partner about this. He says he would be happy to. I believe him.*
>
> *This is a good one. Please look after it.*
