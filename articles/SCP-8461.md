# SCP-8461

**Item #:** SCP-8461

**Object Class:** Safe

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-8461 is a main-belt asteroid, formerly catalogued as **(██████) 20██ ██**, approximately 3.6 km on its longest axis, in a low-inclination orbit at 2.71 AU. The mining claim on SCP-8461 was purchased from its original registrant by ████████ Belt Holdings, a Foundation front company, in 20██, and is maintained as an active claim under the Ceres Registry to explain the presence of personnel and traffic. Ore shipments of appropriate tonnage are dispatched from SCP-8461 on a quarterly basis. The ore is genuine. It is regolith from the outer 200 metres.

Foundation Outpost Belt-3 is established within the accreted shell of SCP-8461, at the point where the original mining bore broke through. The outpost is staffed by a rotating complement of fourteen personnel under **Project Rounds**, administered jointly by the Department of Extrasolar Affairs and the Department of Anomalous Linguistics.

Access to the interior of SCP-8461-A (the vessel) is restricted to Project Rounds personnel. Access to SCP-8461-B (the memory core) is restricted to the linguistics team and requires two-person integrity. Illumination of SCP-8461-B by any light source other than the calibrated read-heads is prohibited; the core is sensitive to modulated light, and the Foundation is not yet certain that it cannot be written to.

The discovery crew of the mining vessel *Anja Sørlie* (seven persons) have been retained under Foundation contract. Six remain on the project. One has been pensioned at her own request and resides on Ceres. She is under Class-C monitoring and has requested, and receives, a copy of each new translation as it is cleared.

The forward compartment of SCP-8461-A (Addendum 8461-G) is not to be entered except for documentation purposes. Its floor is not to be disturbed.

---

**Description:** SCP-8461 is an asteroid. SCP-8461-A is the spacecraft inside it.

SCP-8461-A is a vessel approximately 3.1 km in length and 640 metres in beam, constructed from a metallic composite that the Foundation has been unable to fully characterise and has provisionally described as "an alloy that has not been given the opportunity to corrode." The vessel is entirely encased in a shell of accreted regolith, dust, and ice between 140 and 260 metres thick. Cosmic-ray exposure dating of the outer shell places the *beginning* of accretion at not less than 1.1 billion years ago. Isotopic ratios in the deepest ice layers are consistent with a considerably older figure. The Foundation's current estimate for the age of the vessel is **"between one and three billion years,"** and the Department of Extrasolar Affairs has advised that this uncertainty is not expected to improve.

SCP-8461-A was built for a crew of approximately 2,400. The Foundation knows this because it has counted the places where they would have been.

The vessel's interior is, in the words of the first survey report, "unfamiliar in every particular and familiar in general." Corridors are corridors: they are sized for a biped slightly shorter than a modern human, with handholds at intervals appropriate to a hand with five digits. Doors are doors. Rooms have floors that agree with the direction of the vessel's original spin gravity. There are seats. The seats are sized for something that sat the way we sit.

The vessel's technology is optical. The Foundation has found no electrical conductor anywhere aboard. Every system that has been identified — data, control, what appears to have been lighting, what appears to have been life support — operates on light: guided through channels in the structure, modulated, switched, and stored. This is why it still works. There is nothing to corrode, nothing to discharge, and nothing to leak. SCP-8461-A has been sitting dark in the belt for over a billion years, and when the Foundation shone a light into it, it woke up.

**SCP-8461-B** is the vessel's memory core: a volume of transparent crystalline material approximately 4.2 m × 4.2 m × 11 m, located at the vessel's centre of mass, in which data is stored holographically as interference patterns throughout the bulk of the medium. The Foundation's estimate of its capacity is on the order of 10²¹ bytes. Approximately 3% of its contents have been recovered. Approximately 0.4% have been translated.

The translated portion is a ship's log.

## Addendum 8461-A — Discovery

SCP-8461 was identified in 20██ by the crew of the *Anja Sørlie*, a seven-person mining tug operating out of Ceres under contract to a mid-sized belt operator. The tug's survey geologist, **Ms. Ingrid ████████**, had flagged the asteroid during a routine radar-density pass because its bulk density was "wrong in a way I couldn't make into a rock." Her survey note, reproduced from the tug's log:

> *Density profile has a shell and a core. Shell is normal regolith/ice mix, roughly 200m. Core is dense, uniform, and has edges. Rocks do not have edges. Recommend we bore it, because I would like to know what has edges.*

The tug's captain, on the strength of the density profile alone, filed a claim and authorised a test bore. The bore broke through into a void at 211 metres. The void was a corridor.

The crew's initial exploration was recorded on helmet cameras and has been reviewed in full by the Foundation. It is, in the Project lead's assessment, "the most important hour of footage in the Department's possession, and about forty minutes of it is seven miners saying *what* in various tones."

The tug's systems technician, **Mr. Tomas ████████**, who had worked in optical network maintenance before taking belt contracts, is credited with the first successful interaction with the vessel. He noticed that the panels set into the corridor walls at intervals were not screens — they had no emitters, no pixel structure, no electrical connection — but were, on close inspection, *receivers*: their surfaces were structured to accept light. He aimed his suit's inspection laser at one.

It lit up.

> *I've thought about this a lot since. I want to be clear that I didn't know anything. I didn't have a theory. I had a laser pointer and a panel that looked like it wanted one. That's the whole of it. I pointed it and the panel woke up and showed me something, and I understood exactly none of it, and I stood there for a while feeling like I'd knocked on a door.*

The crew withdrew after four hours and reported the find through the Ceres Registry as an "anomalous structural inclusion." The Registry filing was intercepted by a Foundation asset within eleven minutes. The claim was purchased within the week. The crew were made an offer of employment that the captain later described as "unrefusable, and also, to be fair, generous."

## Addendum 8461-B — The Vessel

Project Rounds has surveyed approximately 60% of SCP-8461-A's interior. Summary findings:

**Layout.** The vessel is organised around a central spine containing the memory core, with habitation rings fore and aft and a large forward compartment of uncertain function (Addendum 8461-G). The habitation rings contain approximately 2,400 individual compartments that the Foundation has, after some debate, elected to call cabins. Each contains a sleeping surface, a seat, storage, and an optical panel. They are small. They are, several surveyors have independently remarked, *cosy* — a word the Project lead has allowed to stand in the reports on the grounds that it is accurate.

**Ergonomics.** The vessel was built by and for bipeds. Ceiling heights average 2.1 m. Seats accommodate a pelvis. Handholds are sized for a grip. Control surfaces are positioned for a standing operator with eyes approximately 1.5 m above the floor. The Foundation's biomechanics team has reconstructed the builders' body plan from the vessel's ergonomics alone, before any imagery was recovered, and produced a figure that was — the team's report uses the word — "unsettlingly ordinary."

**Function.** SCP-8461-A does not appear to have been a warship, a colony ship, or a cargo vessel. It has no identifiable weapons, no cryogenic or stasis facilities, and modest cargo volume relative to its size. It has very large volumes given over to what the Foundation has identified as *meeting spaces*: halls, galleries, rooms with seating arranged to face other seating. It has kitchens. It has what appear to be gardens, long dead. The Foundation's assessment is that SCP-8461-A was built to carry a great many people in comfort for a very long time, to places where they would be received.

**Remains.** There are none. The vessel contains no biological material of any kind. This is not surprising at the age in question; nothing organic survives a billion years in vacuum. But the Foundation notes that there is also no *residue* — no mineralised trace, no discolouration, no debris in any of the 2,400 cabins — of the kind that even fully decayed remains would leave. The cabins are empty in the way a hotel room is empty. Whoever was aboard was not in their cabins at the end.

## Addendum 8461-C — The Core

SCP-8461-B is read by directing coherent light of a specific wavelength through the crystal at a specific angle and recording the interference pattern that emerges. The vessel's own read-heads — optical assemblies positioned around the core — were intact and, once the Foundation understood what they were, functional. The Foundation has built its own in parallel and does not use the originals for routine reads.

Recovery has proceeded in three stages, each of which took longer than the last.

**Decoding** — learning how the interference patterns represent data — took eleven months and was accomplished largely by a single Foundation researcher working with an AI-assisted toolkit of the kind described elsewhere in this archive. The core's encoding is efficient, elegant, and entirely alien, and the researcher's report describes the moment it resolved as "watching noise become handwriting."

**Structure** — learning how the data is organised — took a further eight months. The core is not a single archive. It is thousands of them, cross-referenced, timestamped, and indexed in a way that suggests it was meant to be *browsed*.

The crew did not keep time in days or years. There was no day aboard; there was no sun. The log's base unit is the vessel's own operational rhythm — a period the Foundation has calibrated at approximately 26 hours from the spin-gravity logs — and its larger unit is a fixed count of these, apparently a maintenance interval, corresponding to roughly fourteen months. The linguistics team renders these as **[cycle]** and **[long-cycle]** respectively, and does not convert them within translated text. Where this file gives a figure in days, years, or decades outside a quoted entry, it is the Foundation's conversion, not the crew's.

**Translation** — reading it — is ongoing, and will not conclude in the lifetime of anyone currently assigned. The vessel's language is the linguistics team's primary work. Its script is recovered from the panels; its phonology from a large body of what appear to be audio recordings; its semantics from the correspondence between log entries and the imagery that accompanies them. The team's confidence in its translations is graded, and only entries at the two highest grades are cleared for the file.

The first cleared entry, translated in the twenty-third month, was a maintenance note. It recorded the replacement of a component in a corridor light. The corridor was identified. The light was found. The replacement component was still in it.

## Addendum 8461-D — The Log

SCP-8461-A was, the Foundation now believes, a vessel whose purpose was to *visit*.

The log records a voyage of at least four hundred years' duration (some 340 [long-cycles], by the vessel's own reckoning), on a route that returns repeatedly to the same worlds at intervals of decades — a circuit. The crew's name for the vessel translates, at the highest confidence grade, as *"the one that comes round,"* and Project personnel have come to call it *Rounds*, as a doctor makes rounds. The vessel went from world to world, stayed a while, and moved on, and came back.

The log records **three hundred and seventeen** distinct inhabited worlds visited over the translated portion. It records the vessel's crew being received, housed, fed, shown things, and sent on with cargo. It records disputes, deaths aboard, births aboard, a long section that the linguistics team believes is a recipe, and a number of entries concerning a leak in the aft habitation ring that recurs, unresolved, over sixty years. It is, the Project lead has written, "the administrative record of a large institution doing a job it was good at, kept by people who mostly liked the job."

Representative entries, at high translation confidence:

> *Arrived [world 44]. They have moved the landing ground since last time and did not tell us. [Name] says this is the third time. We were received well. They have a new [word — probably a musical form] and insisted on performing it, all of it. Departure delayed by two [cycles] on account of this. Crew morale good.*

> *[World 112]. The [people of 112] have had a hard interval — a sickness — and asked us to carry word to [world 118] and [world 121], whose people are related to them. We have agreed. [Name] has recorded their message and will not let anyone hear it until we arrive. This is correct.*

> *[World 201]. First visit. They had not seen a ship before and were afraid. We stayed in orbit four [cycles] and let them look at us. On the fifth [cycle] they sent up a small craft with two people in it, who came aboard and ate with us. They had bread. It was not our bread but it was bread. We are calling this a good first visit.*

> *Aft ring leak, again.*

The log contains imagery. The imagery contains the crew.

## Addendum 8461-E — The Crew

The Foundation has recovered several thousand images of SCP-8461-A's crew, and a smaller number of images of the peoples of the worlds they visited. The images are stored in the core as holographic volumes and are, when correctly read, three-dimensional and in colour.

The crew of SCP-8461-A were bipedal. They had two arms, two legs, a head, two eyes, a nose, a mouth, two ears, and five fingers on each hand. They were approximately 1.6 m tall. They had hair. They had the range of skin tones one would expect in a population with a long history of sunlight. They smiled with their mouths.

They were not human. The differences are consistent, and are of the kind a palaeoanthropologist would recognise — a heavier brow, a longer face, a differently set jaw, differences in the proportion of limb to torso. The Foundation's biological team has described the crew as standing in roughly the relation to modern humans that an earlier hominin would — "not us, but close enough that you would cross the room to look, and then stop."

The team was, at this stage of the work, prepared to describe this as a coincidence: a remarkable, distressing, but ultimately statistical convergence. Two arms and two legs are a good design. Eyes go at the front. It could happen.

Then the linguistics team cleared the images of the peoples of the three hundred and seventeen worlds.

They are all this shape.

Not identical. The peoples of the log vary in stature, in colouring, in proportion, in the details of the face. Some are described in the log as unusually tall, or unusually pale, or as having "the old eyes." But in every image recovered, from every world, the beings SCP-8461-A's crew were received by, ate with, traded with, and carried messages between were built to the same plan as the crew — and as us — and stood upright, and looked at the camera.

The crew of SCP-8461-A did not find this remarkable. There is no entry in the log that comments on it. The log describes the peoples of the worlds the way a ship's log describes ports: the landing ground has moved, the food is good, the [musical form] is long. It does not describe them as *like us*. It does not, so far as the Foundation can tell, possess the concept of a people who would not be.

The Foundation has, after eighteen months of review, been unable to produce a model of convergent evolution that accounts for this. The Department of Biological Anomalies' formal position is that the galaxy, at the time the log was kept, was inhabited by a large number of peoples who were all, to within the range of variation seen among hominins, **the same shape**, and that the Foundation does not know why, and that the Foundation is one of them.

## Addendum 8461-F — The Last Circuit

The log changes in its final translated section, covering approximately the last forty years of the voyage.

The vessel arrives at worlds it has visited before and finds them empty.

The entries are brief. They are not, at first, alarmed. The first such world is recorded as *"no reply; no landing ground lights; we did not go down."* The second, some years later, as *"[world 87] is quiet. We stayed in orbit two [cycles]. There is nothing wrong with the world. There is no one on it."* The third, fourth, and fifth follow at shortening intervals. By the tenth, the entries have acquired a word the linguistics team has been unable to translate at any confidence, which the log uses as a noun and which the crew apparently understood without explanation. The Foundation renders it as **[the quiet]**.

> *[World 112] is [the quiet]. We were carrying a message here. [Name] has been to the recording room and has not come out.*

> *[World 44]. The landing ground is where it was last time. The lights are off. We went down. The [musical form] hall is intact. The instruments are in it. There is nothing wrong. There is no one. We have been here eleven [cycles], which is longer than we have ever stayed, because no one has told us to go.*

> *[World 201]. Their small craft is on the ground where they kept it. We did not go down. We did not want to see whether the bread was still there.*

The log does not record what [the quiet] is. It does not record the crew seeing it, or fleeing it, or fighting it. It records only its results, and the vessel's route through them: a circuit that becomes a search, and a search that becomes, in the last entries, something the Foundation has been reluctant to name.

The vessel leaves its circuit. It travels, the log says, "toward the young ones" — a phrase the linguistics team interprets, with moderate confidence, as *systems with worlds not yet inhabited*. It arrives in a system with a warm wet planet whose atmosphere, the log notes with something the translators read as tenderness, "is changing — there is [oxygen] in it now, more than last time; something is breathing."

That planet was Earth, approximately two billion years ago, during the period a modern geologist would call the Great Oxidation.

The vessel parked in the belt.

> *We will stay here. There is no one to visit and no one to carry word to, and the young world below is not ready and will not be for longer than we have. But it is breathing, and we would rather wait near something that is breathing. [Name] says we are the last of the rounds. She may be right. We have shut down what we can and we are gathering forward.*

That is the penultimate entry.

The final entry is one word, at the highest translation confidence. It is the untranslated noun.

## Addendum 8461-G — The Forward Compartment

The forward compartment of SCP-8461-A is the largest single space aboard: a hall approximately 180 m in length, with a floor sloped gently upward toward the forward bulkhead, which is featureless. It contains no seating. It contains no equipment. It was, the Foundation believes, the space in which the vessel's entire complement could assemble.

The floor of the forward compartment carries **two thousand, four hundred and eleven** faint discolourations, each approximately the size of a seated person, arranged in orderly rows, all oriented toward the forward bulkhead.

They are the only trace of the crew anywhere aboard. The Foundation's materials team has established that the discolourations are not remains, and not the residue of remains. They are a change in the surface of the floor itself, of a kind produced by prolonged contact with something at a slightly different temperature. They are the marks left by 2,411 people sitting in the same place for a long time.

There is nothing on the forward bulkhead. There is no door in it. The Foundation has scanned it to a depth of forty metres. It is hull.

They sat down, facing the same way, and waited for something. Whether it came, the Foundation does not know. The floor does not record what they were looking at. It records only that they were all looking.

## Addendum 8461-H — Assessment

The following assessment was submitted to the O5 Council by the Project Rounds lead, Dr. ████ A████, and is reproduced in part.

> *I want to set out what we know and what we do not.*
>
> *We know that between one and three billion years ago, the galaxy was full of people, and that they were our shape. We know that a large vessel of one such people spent centuries going round and visiting the others, and that the visits were, on the whole, kind. We know that something happened to those peoples — world by world, over decades — that left their worlds intact and their landing grounds lit and their instruments in the hall, and left no one to play them. We know that the crew of this vessel called it by a word that we cannot translate and that they did not need to explain to each other. We know that they came here, to a world with nothing on it but the first breath of oxygen, because they wanted to wait near something alive. And we know that they went forward, and sat down together, and looked at a wall.*
>
> *We do not know what [the quiet] is. We do not know whether it is a thing that happens to peoples of our shape, or a thing that was done to them, or a thing that they did. We do not know whether it came here. We do not know whether it left. We do not know whether a world of bacteria was beneath its notice, or whether it noticed, and has been waiting for the world to grow up.*
>
> *We are the same shape as the people in the log. I have looked at a woman's face, recorded in light, from two billion years ago, and she had my mother's nose. I do not know what that means and I have stopped pretending to. The biological team's phrase is "the galaxy makes this shape." I would put it differently. I would say: the galaxy made this shape, many times, and then it stopped, and we are the first time it has made it again since.*
>
> *The people in the log did not know they were the last. They found out one world at a time. They wrote it down in case someone came after them, and then they parked next to the only thing in the system that was breathing, and waited. I think they were waiting for us. I do not think they expected us to look like them. I think, if they had known, they would have found it unremarkable. They would have moved the landing ground and not told us, and shown us the hall, and given us bread.*
>
> *Recommendation: continue translation. Continue survey. Do not disturb the forward compartment. And — I am aware this is not within my remit — begin listening, more carefully than we have been, for anything in the galaxy that is quiet in a way it should not be.*

The recommendation was accepted in full. The final clause has been referred to the Department of Extrasolar Affairs, which has confirmed that it was already listening, and has been for some time, and has not found anything, and would like it noted that this is not the same as there being nothing to find.

## Addendum 8461-I — Ms. ████████

Ms. Ingrid ████████, the survey geologist who first flagged SCP-8461, left Project Rounds in its third year and lives on Ceres. She receives each cleared translation as it is released. She has written to the Project lead once, after receiving the entry concerning World 201. Her letter is reproduced with her permission.

> *Thank you for sending these. I read them on the observation deck, where you can see the belt, which is probably sentimental of me.*
>
> *I want to say one thing, as the person who found it, in case it's useful. Everyone on the project talks about the last entries — the quiet, the hall, the wall. I understand why. But I've read the whole log now, every cleared entry, and the thing I keep coming back to isn't the end. It's the four hundred years before it. It's that they had a ship the size of a city and they used it to go round and check on everyone. That they carried messages. That they stayed two extra cycles for the music.*
>
> *Whatever happened to them, they were the sort of people who did that. I'd like that to be in the file somewhere, in case the file is the only thing that ends up remembering them.*
>
> *And I'd like it noted that I bored into a rock because it had edges, and I'd do it again.*

It is in the file.
