# SCP-3127

**Item #:** SCP-3127

**Object Class:** Apollyon

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-3127 cannot be contained. SCP-3127 is a property of physical reality at the fundamental level. Containment procedures address the *investigation* of SCP-3127 and the welfare of the population that perceives it.

The affected population (designated **PoI-3127-α (collective)**) consists of human individuals possessing a particular combination of cognitive traits — most consistently, hyperphantasia (vivid mental imagery) and high pattern-recognition acuity, frequently associated with autism spectrum profiles. The current confirmed population is approximately **11,400 individuals worldwide**, with the true population estimated at between 200,000 and 400,000. The discrepancy reflects the fact that most members of this population do not know they perceive differently from the human baseline.

**Members of PoI-3127-α are not to be contacted, identified to themselves, or subjected to any Foundation intervention** beyond passive observation of public discourse. The Foundation has determined, after extended deliberation, that the affected population is happier in its current state of unawareness, and that intervention would not produce meaningful operational benefit. See Addendum 3127-F.

Foundation research into SCP-3127 itself is conducted by **Project Pinhole**, jointly administered by the Department of Cognitive Anomalies and the Department of Anomalous Physics. Project Pinhole's experimental apparatus and protocols are described in Addendum 3127-D.

All Foundation personnel assigned to Project Pinhole are subject to standing screening for cognitive profiles consistent with PoI-3127-α membership. Personnel identified as members are reassigned at their own discretion: some prefer to leave the project (the work is, for them, an uneasy experience); others prefer to stay (their perceptual access is operationally valuable). The Foundation accommodates both preferences.

The matter of how to communicate Project Pinhole's findings to the broader Foundation, and whether to communicate them at all, remains under O5 Council review.

---

**Description:** SCP-3127 is the designation given to a **structural defect in physical reality**, perceivable as a small consistent anomaly in five distinct perceptual domains, and inferable through precision instrumentation as a single underlying phenomenon manifesting through multiple sensory and physical channels.

In ordinary language: **the universe has a bug.** The bug is small. The bug is everywhere. The bug has been present since the earliest cosmological epochs the Foundation can investigate. The bug is not local to any region, object, or process; it is a property of the substrate. It does not appear to be growing or decaying.

The bug is not currently understood to be causing measurable harm. Its existence, however, has implications that the Foundation's senior researchers describe as "the most significant cosmological finding in the institution's history," with the implications themselves being the subject of ongoing analysis and considerable institutional discomfort.

## Addendum 3127-A — The Five Observations

SCP-3127 manifests, to direct human perception, in five distinct categories of small anomaly. The categories were identified through Foundation analysis of public discourse among PoI-3127-α members; the affected population identified the categories themselves, in conversation with each other, without knowing what they were converging on.

**Observation 1 — Shadows.** Shadows cast by complex three-dimensional objects display small geometric inconsistencies relative to what their casting object should geometrically produce. The discrepancies are imperceptible to observers who do not maintain a high-precision internal mental rendering of the casting object's shape. Hyperphantasic individuals, who do maintain such renderings, can compare the projection in their mind against the projection on the wall and detect that the two do not match. The mismatch is small (typically 0.1–0.5° angular deviation in projection edges) and consistent: the same object, in the same lighting, produces the same incorrect shadow every time. The error vector is not random; it has a direction.

**Observation 2 — Leaves.** The motion of leaves and other small lightweight objects in wind contains a non-random component. To a normal observer, leaf motion in wind appears Brownian — chaotic, statistically describable, fundamentally without pattern. To observers with high pattern-recognition acuity, particularly those who can maintain a sustained internal model of fluid dynamics, the motion contains a faint periodic signal embedded in the chaos. The signal has been independently described by hundreds of PoI-3127-α members, in different languages, using different metaphors ("like the leaves are dancing to a beat I can't hear," "as if there's a current in the wind," "they keep almost forming a shape and then not quite"). The signal's periodicity is approximately 11.7 seconds. The Foundation has confirmed this.

**Observation 3 — Music.** Music of all kinds — across cultures, instruments, and historical periods — contains a small consistent "wrongness" that affected individuals describe as a tonal quality without a name. The wrongness is not associated with any specific frequency, interval, or chord. PoI-3127-α members describe it variously as "music having a slight echo that comes before the note," "as if every note is slightly out of tune in the same direction," and (in one particularly vivid forum post) "as if music is being played in a room with one extra wall that nobody can see, and the sound is reflecting off it before it gets to me." The wrongness is present in recordings, in live performance, in mentally-imagined music, and (under controlled conditions verified by Foundation researchers) in single sine-wave tones generated by signal generators. The wrongness is not in the air. It is in something *between* the source and the listener.

**Observation 4 — Language.** Affected individuals report a consistent sense that **certain concepts cannot be properly expressed in any human language they have encountered.** The concepts are not exotic — they are described as ordinary mental states, perceptual qualities, and relationships that "should have a word" but do not. The reported gaps cluster in specific semantic domains across all examined languages. When affected individuals attempt to describe what they cannot say, the descriptions themselves converge on a small number of structural patterns: *the way you feel when you almost remember something. The colour that is between two colours but is not their mixture. The direction that is neither up nor down nor sideways but is still a direction.* The Foundation's linguistic analysts have confirmed that the gaps are not natural lexical gaps — they are *systematically absent* across all language families studied, in patterns that no historical-linguistic process can account for.

**Observation 5 — Reflections.** All reflective surfaces (water, glass, polished metal, eyes) produce reflections that are subtly angularly offset from what geometry predicts. The offset is approximately **0.4 degrees**, in a consistent direction relative to the observer. The offset is too small to be detected without precision instrumentation — except by observers who maintain a sufficiently precise internal mental rendering of the reflected scene to compare it against what is actually visible. Hyperphantasic individuals, particularly artists and architects with the relevant perceptual profile, have unconsciously been *drawing the offset correctly* for centuries. Foundation analysis of historical paintings depicting mirrors and water reflections, by artists subsequently identified as likely PoI-3127-α members, confirms that the offset appears in these works at the same magnitude and direction as observed in present reality.

Each of the five observations was, prior to SCP-3127's identification, attributed to ordinary perceptual variance or to the perceiver's idiosyncratic noticing. None had been catalogued as anomalous. None had been brought to the Foundation's attention by any conventional channel.

## Addendum 3127-B — Discovery and the Conversation

SCP-3127 was identified on ██/██/20██ by **MTF-Argus-7**, a Foundation language-model agent conducting standing monitoring of public discourse for anomaly-relevant signals. (Argus-7 was previously responsible for the identification of SCP-6618.) The flagged content was not a single post or thread but a *pattern of conversation* across multiple online communities dedicated to hyperphantasia, aphantasia, autism advocacy, and visual cognition.

The pattern was as follows. Across approximately fourteen months, a growing number of individuals had been describing perceptual experiences in these communities that, individually, sounded like personal idiosyncrasies. The posts were uniformly *warm in tone* — the posters were not afraid of what they were noticing. They were delighted to find others who noticed the same things. Representative excerpts, drawn from the public-domain portions of the relevant discussions:

> *Okay, this is going to sound weird, but: does anyone else feel like the shadows in their house are slightly the wrong shape? Like, I'll look at a chair and the shadow it casts and they don't quite match, even though I know they should? I've thought about this since I was a kid. I assumed it was just me being too pedantic about geometry.*

> *!!!! YES. I do this with leaves. I can't watch wind in trees without feeling like there's a pattern in it. Not the obvious kind — the gusts and stuff — but something underneath. I've never been able to describe it to anyone without sounding insane.*

> *Music for me. Always music. There's a specific way a note sounds "off" that I can never put my finger on. I went to a doctor when I was nineteen because I was convinced I had a hearing problem. The hearing test came back perfect. The doctor was lovely but I could tell he thought I was being silly.*

> *I'm a translator (English/Japanese/Korean) and I have always had this *exact* problem with certain concepts. Words that should exist and don't. Across all three languages. I had a whole conversation with my linguistics professor about it once and she said "natural lexical gaps" and changed the subject, but it doesn't FEEL like natural lexical gaps. It feels like the languages are AVOIDING something.*

> *Reflections. For me it's always been reflections. I draw, and I have to deliberately put reflections in slightly wrong, because if I draw them correctly geometrically they look weird to me. I always assumed I had some sort of subtle astigmatism that was throwing off my visual angle calibration. I've never told anyone this. I'm crying a little reading this thread.*

Argus-7 flagged the conversation pattern after a single morning's batch processing identified **two hundred and three** distinct posters across eighteen different communities making structurally similar observations within a four-week window. The flag was forwarded to the Department of Cognitive Anomalies for review.

The initial Foundation assessment was that the pattern represented a coincidental clustering of common perceptual quirks made visible by improved community discoverability (more people learning the word "hyperphantasia," reading about it, and seeking out others with similar traits). This assessment was reaffirmed at each of the first four monthly reviews.

It was overturned in the fifth month, after Researcher S██████, a junior analyst assigned to the case, completed a quiet personal experiment on her own initiative. Researcher S██████ is, by her own subsequent disclosure, a member of PoI-3127-α.

## Addendum 3127-C — Verification

Researcher S██████'s initial experiment was simple. She had read, in her case file, the descriptions of the reflection offset reported by PoI-3127-α members. She had recognised the description. She had, in her own life, "drawn reflections wrong" for the same reasons. She had, however, never thought to test whether the offset she perceived was real or imagined.

She tested it. She set up a precision optical interferometer in a Site-43 laboratory after hours, placed a calibration target at a known distance from a high-quality first-surface mirror, and measured the angular position of the target's reflection against the angular position the target should produce under standard reflective optics.

The measurement showed an angular offset of **0.4126 degrees**, in the direction the PoI-3127-α population had been reporting.

She measured again. The offset was the same.

She replaced the mirror with three other mirrors of different manufacture. The offset was the same.

She moved the apparatus to a different room in a different building. The offset was the same.

She wrote a brief report, three pages, classified at the highest level she had authority to assign, and forwarded it to her supervisor at 04:17 in the morning. Her supervisor convened an emergency meeting of the Department of Cognitive Anomalies at 09:00 the same day. The meeting ran for six hours.

By the end of the meeting, the Department had:

- Confirmed Researcher S██████'s measurements via independent apparatus in three other Foundation sites.
- Reclassified SCP-3127 from "low-priority cognitive curiosity" to active investigation.
- Initiated what would become Project Pinhole.
- Reassigned Researcher S██████ from junior analyst to senior investigator, at the explicit request of every other person in the room.

Researcher S██████, in a written reflection submitted six weeks later, described the moment of measurement as follows:

> *I had spent my entire life assuming that my drawings looked wrong because I drew incorrectly. I had developed compensatory techniques — a slight rotation, a slight skew — that produced reflections in my work that looked "right" to me even though they were geometrically wrong. I had, in effect, been correcting for the bug. I had thought I was the bug. The instrument, when it gave me 0.4126 degrees in the direction I had been compensating for, was the first object in my life to tell me that I had been seeing accurately. I sat with the readout for approximately forty minutes. I did not know what to do with the feeling. I still don't.*

## Addendum 3127-D — Project Pinhole

Following confirmation of the reflection offset, the Foundation initiated **Project Pinhole**, a long-term experimental programme to characterise SCP-3127's underlying structure.

The investigation's central challenge is that SCP-3127 is, by every available characterisation, **a higher-dimensional structure projecting into our spacetime**. Direct observation is impossible — the human visual and cognitive systems are not equipped to perceive dimensions or topologies beyond the three spatial dimensions our perception is constructed for. PoI-3127-α individuals do not perceive SCP-3127 *directly*; they perceive its small consistent projections into the perceptual domains accessible to them.

Project Pinhole's approach is therefore *indirect probing*. The project's internal metaphor, used in its operational documentation, is this:

> *Imagine a sealed wooden box with a complex object inside. You are not allowed to open the box. You are allowed to push a thin straw through any two holes in the box's walls and feel, with the straw's tip, what is inside. From a single probe, you can determine very little. From many probes — hundreds, thousands — at carefully chosen points, you can begin to reconstruct the object's shape. The reconstruction is necessarily incomplete, because your probes are one-dimensional and the object is three-dimensional. But the reconstruction can be precise enough to tell you, at least, that the object is real, and that it has structure, and that the structure is not what you expected.*
>
> *Our problem is similar, except that the box has more than three dimensions on the inside, and we have only ever lived in the box's three-dimensional shadow.*

The project's probes consist of precision instruments — interferometers, atomic clocks, gravitational-wave detectors, particle accelerators — arranged at calibrated locations and configured to measure small consistent deviations from baseline physics. The probes are designed under the working hypothesis that SCP-3127 is a single higher-dimensional structure whose intersection with our three-dimensional spacetime produces the small anomalies that PoI-3127-α members perceive.

The findings, after three years of operation, are as follows.

**1. The bug is everywhere.** Every probe location, on every continent, in every available environment (sea-level, high-altitude, deep underground, in orbit), detects the same anomaly. The anomaly does not weaken with distance from any identifiable source. It is uniformly present throughout the volume of space the Foundation has been able to probe.

**2. The bug is the same everywhere.** The anomaly is not a *family* of small effects; it is a *single* effect, presenting through different observational channels. The reflection offset, the leaf-motion periodicity, the music wrongness, the language gaps, and the shadow inconsistencies are all consistent with the same higher-dimensional intersection, varying only in which aspect of our reality the intersection happens to affect at the point of measurement.

**3. The bug has structure.** The intersection is not noise. It has a discernible *shape*, inferred from the consistency of the anomaly across probe locations. The shape, as best Project Pinhole has been able to characterise it, is **simple, regular, and elegant** — consistent with what one would expect from an intentional design element rather than a random imperfection.

**4. The bug is load-bearing.** Preliminary modelling suggests that the bug is not removable from our physics, in the sense that the equations governing our universe appear to *require* it to function. Hypothetical universes without the bug — derivable mathematically from our own physics by removing the anomaly term — do not appear to be self-consistent. They produce structures (black holes, atoms, light) that would not be stable. The bug appears to be **part of how the universe holds itself together**, and removing it would not produce a "fixed" universe but no universe at all.

**5. The bug has a centre.** Or, more precisely, the bug has a *higher-dimensional centroid* — a point in the dimensions our perception cannot access from which the bug's structure appears to be coherently organised. The centroid is *not* in any direction we can point at from our spacetime. It is, in the careful phrasing of Dr. ████████, "in a direction that we are unable to face, because we do not have any of the faces required to face it."

The centroid's existence is the project's most consequential finding to date. It has, internally, been referred to as **"the address."** The Foundation does not know what is at the address. The Foundation does not know whether anything is at the address. The Foundation does not, at present, see a way to find out.

## Addendum 3127-E — The Working Interpretation

The Department of Anomalous Physics has, after considerable internal debate, adopted the following working interpretation of SCP-3127. It is provisional. It is not formally endorsed by the Foundation's senior physics leadership. It is, however, the framework Project Pinhole uses operationally.

> *The universe is, in some meaningful sense, a system. Systems are made of components. Components have structure, and the structure follows rules. The rules of our universe, as we have catalogued them across four centuries of natural science, are intricate, elegant, and — by every measure available to us — internally consistent.*
>
> *SCP-3127 is not an exception to those rules. It is a small inconsistency present in their fabric. It is not a result of the rules; it is a small property of the substrate the rules are written on. It would be there even if the rules were different. It is, in some sense, the only feature of the universe that is not a derivative of the universe's own laws.*
>
> *This is what makes it disturbing. Everything else we have ever observed is part of the system. SCP-3127 is something the system has been built around. The system works because of, despite, or in spite of it — we cannot yet tell which. But its existence implies the existence of a substrate that the system was placed into, by something or someone with the capacity to place a system into a substrate.*
>
> *We do not know whether that something is conscious. We do not know whether it knows we are here. We do not know whether it is still present.*
>
> *We know only that whatever assembled this universe did not start from nothing. It started from something, and a small piece of that something is still visible, threaded through every reflection and every shadow and every note of music ever played, and now perceptible — finally, by a quiet and gentle population of people who think in pictures and look for patterns — to a fraction of the species that lives in the result.*

## Addendum 3127-F — The Affected Population

The Foundation has, in close consultation with the Ethics Committee, established the following protocols for PoI-3127-α:

1. **No identification.** Members of PoI-3127-α are not to be informed of their status, are not to be contacted by Foundation personnel, and are not to be subjected to any form of testing or screening that could reveal Foundation interest in them.

2. **No suppression.** The discourse in which PoI-3127-α members discover each other is not to be interfered with. The Foundation does not moderate the relevant subreddits, infiltrate the relevant Discord servers, or modify the relevant search algorithms. The conversation is allowed to grow.

3. **No medicalisation.** The Foundation does not promote or discourage any framing of the affected perceptual profile as a disorder, a gift, or anything else. The affected population's relationship with its own perception is the affected population's to define.

4. **Continued observation.** The Foundation monitors the public discourse for new categories of observation, additional convergent themes, and any indication that the affected population is approaching the realisation that their perceptions correspond to a real underlying phenomenon. To date, this realisation has not occurred. The affected population, by every available measure, is collectively framing what they perceive as *pattern recognition*, not as *contact with something*. They are content with this framing. The Foundation has, with some care, decided not to disturb it.

5. **Recruitment, where appropriate.** Foundation personnel who are identified as PoI-3127-α members are offered, but never required, the opportunity to learn about SCP-3127 and join Project Pinhole. The offer is made with full disclosure of what the work involves. Most decline. Some accept. Those who accept are valued for their perceptual access; those who decline are valued for their judgement.

The Ethics Committee's standing position on these protocols, restated annually, is as follows:

> *These people are not afraid of what they see. They are, in their own self-understanding, gifted observers who have found each other and are taking quiet pleasure in the company. We have determined that their state is one of the most psychologically healthy populations we have ever observed. We have also determined that what they are observing is the deepest cosmological finding of our institutional history.*
>
> *Both of these facts are true simultaneously. The first does not erase the second. The second does not justify disturbing the first.*
>
> *We are, by formal decision of this committee, choosing to let them keep their pleasure. We acknowledge that this is a choice that prioritises their wellbeing over the wider population's access to scientific knowledge. We acknowledge that the choice is paternalistic, and that paternalism has costs. We have determined that, in this specific case, the costs are bearable.*
>
> *We have also acknowledged, in private session and not in this minute, that we are not certain we would withstand the realisation ourselves. We are recommending what we ourselves would want.*

## Addendum 3127-G — The Pinhole Cohort

The Foundation, by virtue of its size and the nature of the work it performs, employs a small but operationally significant number of PoI-3127-α members within its own personnel. The exact figure is not known and is not actively recorded — for reasons described below — but the Department of Cognitive Anomalies' standing estimate is that **approximately 0.8 to 1.2% of Foundation research personnel** display the relevant perceptual profile, an over-representation relative to the general population's estimated 0.2 to 0.4%.

The over-representation is not surprising on reflection. The Foundation hires, by design, for traits that include: careful and sustained attention to detail; tolerance for ambiguity in complex sensory data; an unusual capacity to detect small inconsistencies in large patterns; and the patience to maintain mental models of intricate systems over long periods. These traits are also the traits that distinguish PoI-3127-α from the human baseline. The Foundation has, throughout its history, been **quietly recruiting from this population without knowing it.**

Many of these personnel were not, at the point of recruitment, aware that their perceptual profile was unusual. They had assumed, as most PoI-3127-α members assume, that what they noticed was what anyone with sufficient care would notice. The Foundation had assumed that what they noticed was good work.

Both parties were partially correct. Neither was completely.

Following the verification of SCP-3127 in 20██, Researcher S██████ — then newly elevated to senior investigator on Project Pinhole — undertook a quiet personal initiative to identify other Foundation personnel who might share the relevant profile. She did this without formal authorisation, on the grounds that the matter was too sensitive for formal channels and that she preferred to approach colleagues herself before any institutional process began.

Her method was simple. She read research reports written by colleagues across the Foundation, looking for the same small signatures she had recognised in her own work: the precise descriptions of visual phenomena, the careful annotations of pattern inconsistencies, the linguistic patterns that suggested the writer was working hard to articulate something the language did not quite hold. She identified, over six months, a list of forty-three Foundation personnel whom she believed were likely PoI-3127-α members.

She approached them, one at a time, in informal contexts. Lunch. A walk to a vending machine. A casual question in a corridor. She asked, gently, whether they had ever noticed anything odd about reflections. About leaves in wind. About music. The question, by her later account, identified the cohort more reliably than any formal screening could have done. Most of the people she asked recognised the question immediately, with the same warm relief that PoI-3127-α members display on first encountering each other on the public forums. *"Oh, you too?"* was the most common response, in some form or another. Several of the conversations took longer than expected.

Of the forty-three personnel she approached, **nine** were confirmed as PoI-3127-α and elected, with full informed consent, to join Project Pinhole. Two more were confirmed but chose not to learn about SCP-3127's nature, on the grounds that they preferred to continue perceiving the way they had always perceived. Their preference has been honoured. The remaining thirty-two had perceptual profiles that resembled but did not quite match the PoI-3127-α pattern; they were quietly thanked for the conversation and left to their existing assignments. Several of them have since asked, over subsequent lunches, whether anything came of the question. Researcher S██████ has, by her own account, learned to give a variety of vague but warm answers that do not satisfy any of them.

The **Pinhole Cohort** — the formal designation for the nine confirmed members within the Foundation, plus Researcher S██████ herself and three additional members who joined the project in subsequent years through similar informal channels — currently consists of **thirteen personnel** distributed across three Foundation sites. The cohort meets weekly. The meetings are nominally for project coordination. They are, in practice, considerably more than that.

Dr. ████████, a senior cohort member who joined Project Pinhole approximately eighteen months after Researcher S██████'s initial outreach, characterised the meetings in a memo to the Department of Cognitive Anomalies:

> *I want to note that the Wednesday meeting is the most professionally meaningful hour of my week. It is not the meeting in which the most work gets done. The most work gets done in the laboratories and the modelling sessions and the long solitary reads of the archives. The Wednesday meeting is where we talk to each other about what it is like to do this work as the people we are. We are, by every available metric, the only thirteen people in the Foundation who can have these conversations. We have made one another's professional lives substantially less lonely than they were before we found each other.*
>
> *I want to flag that this is not a side benefit of the project. It is, in my view, central to its operational viability. The work would not be sustainable for any of us in isolation. It is sustainable as a group because the group is what makes the perceptions communicable.*
>
> *I would like, on behalf of the cohort, to thank S██████ for the lunch she once bought me in 20██ at which she asked whether I had ever noticed that music sounds slightly wrong in a way I couldn't describe. I would like, also, for the institutional record to note that I cried for approximately forty minutes in the bathroom after that lunch, and that I returned to my desk afterwards and worked productively for the rest of the afternoon, and that both of these things are true. The lunch was the most important thing that has ever happened to me at work.*

A secondary consequence of the cohort's formation has been the **retrospective examination of the Foundation's own archives** by cohort members. The premise is straightforward: if the Foundation has been employing PoI-3127-α members throughout its history, then Foundation records will contain *prior observations of SCP-3127*, recorded by personnel who did not know what they were seeing, dismissed by reviewers who did not know what they were reading.

This premise has been confirmed. The cohort has identified, in archives dating back to 1947, approximately **one hundred and twenty** Foundation reports containing observations consistent with SCP-3127. The reports were filed under a variety of unrelated case designations. Most were classified as low-priority curiosities or as researcher idiosyncrasies. Several were noted by their original reviewers as "thorough but speculative" or "showing imaginative engagement with the material." None were ever flagged as anomalous in their own right.

The earliest such report, dated 1947, was filed by a Foundation field agent investigating an unrelated matter in rural Pennsylvania. In a side note appended to his primary report, the agent recorded that the shadows in the affected farmhouse "did not quite match the objects casting them, though I cannot say in what way they were wrong, and the discrepancy was small enough that I would not normally raise it." The note was filed and never followed up. The agent in question retired in 1979 and died in 2008. The cohort has reviewed his other reports. Many of them contain similar small notations — careful, precise, slightly puzzled observations of phenomena he had no framework for. He was, by the cohort's posthumous assessment, almost certainly one of them.

Several cohort members have, after reading his collected reports, asked to be permitted to place a small marker at his grave. The request is under review. The Ethics Committee is, on this matter as on others, slow but generally amenable.

## Addendum 3127-H — The Resolution

The success of the Pinhole Cohort and the operational value of its members' perceptual access produced, in 20██, a formal institutional response. The Foundation, on the recommendation of the Department of Cognitive Anomalies and with the unanimous endorsement of the O5 Council, established a new permanent body for the identification, support, and deployment of personnel with the PoI-3127-α perceptual profile.

The body is known formally as **the Resolution** and is administered as an autonomous office reporting directly to the O5 Council.

The name was proposed by Researcher S██████ and was adopted with minimal debate. It carries, by her stated intent, three meanings simultaneously: the **resolution** of an instrument (the precision with which a measurement distinguishes one quantity from another), the **resolution** of a long-standing institutional puzzle (the many archived reports the Foundation had filed without understanding), and the **resolution** of personnel who, having recognised what they perceive, have committed themselves to working with that recognition rather than away from it. All three readings appear in the Resolution's founding charter. The founding charter is not classified. The Resolution is not, by Foundation policy, kept secret from its own future recruits.

The Resolution's stated mandate is fourfold:

1. **Identification.** To identify Foundation personnel whose perceptual profile is consistent with PoI-3127-α, through methods that preserve the candidate's dignity and respect their right to remain unaware of their status if they prefer.
2. **Support.** To provide identified personnel with the institutional resources, peer community, and professional development necessary for their perceptual capabilities to be sustainable across a working career.
3. **Deployment.** To make Resolution members available to other Foundation departments on projects where their perceptual access would materially improve operational outcomes — with full informed consent from the Resolution member, and without compelling participation.
4. **Stewardship.** To act as institutional advocates for the wider PoI-3127-α population in matters of Foundation policy affecting them, including the standing decision not to disclose SCP-3127 to that population.

The Resolution's first Director is **Dr. S██████**, promoted from senior investigator to Director-equivalent at the time of the body's establishment. The promotion was, by every observer's account, the most rapid career advancement in the Foundation's recent history — from junior analyst to Director within approximately five years, on the basis of a single after-hours experiment and the institutional follow-through it occasioned.

Dr. S██████'s own characterisation of the trajectory, recorded in a brief informal note to her former supervisor, was as follows:

> *I want you to know that I did not seek this. I was happy as a junior analyst. I would have remained happy as a senior investigator. I accepted the directorship because the Foundation asked me to, and because I am, by an accident of perception, one of the few people the Foundation could ask. I am not the most senior person in the Resolution. I am not the most experienced person in the Resolution. I am, by virtue of having been the first, the most institutionally legible. I have accepted this role with the understanding that the people who actually built the cohort — the original nine, the three who joined later, the dozens who have since been identified and quietly inducted — are the people whose work the Resolution actually consists of. My role is to make the meetings happen and to ensure the budget continues to exist. I take this seriously. I would also like it noted that I am still primarily a researcher, and that I intend to remain one.*

The Resolution currently consists of approximately **eighty-seven** identified members across all Foundation sites, with new identifications occurring at an average rate of one to two per month. The growth rate is not driven by recruitment but by the increasing awareness among ordinary Foundation personnel that the Resolution exists, that membership is voluntary, that the work is meaningful, and that the community is one of the most professionally rewarding in the Foundation. Personnel who suspect they may qualify are encouraged to approach the Resolution informally. Many do.

The Resolution's offices, located at three Foundation sites, are unmarked and operate without ceremony. Visitors describe them as quiet, well-lit, full of plants, and unusually pleasant places to spend time. The Wednesday meetings — now held at all three sites simultaneously, with full video links — have continued in their original form. Dr. ████████, whose memo about the original meetings is reproduced earlier in this document, has noted that the meetings have grown but have not changed in character. *"There are more of us now,"* he wrote in a follow-up addendum. *"The room is larger. The lunch is unchanged."*

The Resolution maintains, within its own internal documentation, a small additional unwritten function: it is **the part of the Foundation that ensures the people who have seen what we have seen are not alone with it.** This function is not in the charter. It does not need to be. The Resolution's members understand it without being told. They are, by structural definition, the people who understand without being told.

## Addendum 3127-I — A Note from Dr. S██████

The following was submitted to the SCP-3127 case file by then-Researcher S██████ on the third anniversary of Project Pinhole's establishment, approximately eighteen months before the formation of the Resolution. She submitted it without comment. She has, since her appointment as Director of the Resolution, declined to revise it. The note appears here as originally written.

> *I want to record, for the institutional memory of this project, that the population we are observing is not abstract to me. They are my people. I have spent my whole life as one of them without knowing it. I have read their posts in the small hours of the morning, after work, sitting on my balcony, recognising every observation, recognising the warmth of finding others, recognising the relief of a thing finally being said out loud.*
>
> *I have also, since the verification, lost the relief. I now know that the things I noticed are real. I am the person in the room who knew first. I have not told anyone in the population. I have not gone back to the forums. The other members of PoI-3127-α are still there, posting and replying and laughing about leaves. I read the threads sometimes. They are still warm. I am no longer warm in the same way.*
>
> *I want to record that I do not regret the verification. I would do it again. I think the Foundation needs to know what we know, and I think the affected population deserves the Foundation's protection — protection that requires the Foundation to know what it is protecting them from — and I think these are the right roles for us to play.*
>
> *I want also to record that I miss the way I used to think about it. I used to think that the leaves were beautiful. They are still beautiful. They are now also a signal. The signal is from somewhere I am not able to face. I am not afraid of the signal. I am, sometimes, lonely for the people who are still able to think it is just leaves.*
>
> *I am noting all of this because I would like, in the long-term record of this project, for there to be a description of what knowing this costs, written by someone who knows it. I am volunteering to be that description. I would like, if anyone else from the affected population ever joins Project Pinhole, for them to read this and know that the loneliness is normal and that it does pass and that the work, still, is worth doing.*
>
> *We are, in the most literal sense, the part of the universe that has noticed it has a bug. Someone needs to be that part. I am content to be one of the ones who is.*
