# SCP-4488

**Item #:** SCP-4488

**Object Class:** Euclid

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-4488 cannot be contained by intervention with affected individuals, who are designated **PoI-4488-α (collective).** As of the most recent census (██/██/20██), PoI-4488-α has an estimated active population of **47,000 to 62,000 individuals worldwide**, with a confirmed identified subset of 8,143. The full population cannot be enumerated by direct methods; the upper bound is inferred from event-scale analysis.

Containment of SCP-4488 proceeds along three parallel tracks:

**Track 1 — Event Mitigation.** Foundation embedded assets at major internet infrastructure providers, telecommunications carriers, financial institutions, and selected physical locations monitor for the statistical signatures of SCP-4488 coordination events. Events are mitigated where possible (traffic shaping, transparent queueing, advance scheduling) to prevent operational damage to targeted entities. The mitigation is conducted under cover lines describing "anomalous traffic patterns" without reference to the underlying cause.

**Track 2 — Individual Monitoring.** Identified PoI-4488-α individuals are placed under non-intrusive passive observation. **No direct contact is permitted under any circumstances.** Identified individuals are not informed of their status. The reason is recorded in Addendum 4488-D.

**Track 3 — Source Investigation.** A standing research effort, currently coordinated by the Department of Anomalous Group Behaviour, investigates the origin, mechanism, and possible controlling agency of SCP-4488. The investigation has, over its eight-year history, produced numerous hypotheses and no confirmed answers. The current operative hypothesis space is described in Addendum 4488-E.

Foundation personnel investigating SCP-4488 are subject to standing screening for SCP-5829 markers (cf. SCP-5829 documentation), per a 2023 Ethics Committee directive responding to the unresolved question of whether SCP-4488 is, in some sense, a variant or downstream effect of SCP-5829-Ω. The screening has, to date, returned no positive results. The directive remains in force on a precautionary basis.

---

**Description:** SCP-4488 is the designation given to a population of human individuals who, without their conscious awareness and apparently without any direct communication channel between them, perform coordinated actions in temporal unison against specific targets. The actions taken by SCP-4488 participants are individually mundane — logging into a website, placing a telephone call, visiting a physical location, sending an email, searching a particular phrase — and would not, in isolation, attract attention. The anomaly is in the **coordination**: tens of thousands of individuals, with no known means of communication, performing the same action against the same target within time windows of seconds to minutes.

PoI-4488-α individuals, when interviewed (under cover and without disclosure of the underlying phenomenon), report no awareness of having participated. They do not remember the actions. They do not know the targets. They have no political, professional, or personal connection to the targets they have been used to affect. They are, by every conventional measure, a representative cross-section of the general population in the countries where they reside — distributed across age, gender, ethnicity, education level, income, religious affiliation, occupation, and political alignment in proportions consistent with regional baselines.

The exception is the **targets** of SCP-4488's coordinated actions, which display a distinct pattern. See Addendum 4488-B.

The Foundation does not currently know:

- How PoI-4488-α individuals are recruited.
- How coordination is achieved in the absence of any detectable communication channel.
- What entity, mechanism, or process selects the targets.
- Whether the participants suffer any cumulative cognitive or physiological cost from their unwilling participation.
- Whether SCP-4488 is related to, derived from, or independent of SCP-5829-Ω.

The Foundation also does not currently know whether SCP-4488 is growing because the underlying mechanism is expanding its reach, or because the Foundation's detection capability is improving. It is, with low confidence, believed to be the former.

## Addendum 4488-A — Discovery and the Noise Threshold

SCP-4488 was identified on ██/██/2017 by an automated traffic-pattern monitoring system deployed by the Foundation at a major content delivery network operator. The monitoring system was not designed to detect SCP-4488; it was deployed for an unrelated investigation involving suspected botnet activity associated with a Group of Interest.

The system flagged, on the date of discovery, a 47-second burst of HTTP traffic targeting a specific corporate website (a multinational fossil-fuel company, subsequently designated **Target-4488-001**). The burst originated from approximately 9,200 distinct IP addresses, was visible to the operator as a moderate denial-of-service event, and was attributed at the time to a botnet of compromised consumer devices. Standard mitigation protocols were applied. The traffic subsided. The investigation was closed.

A subsequent retrospective review, conducted in 2019 in response to a separate pattern flagged in telecommunications data, examined the 2017 burst in greater detail. The review established the following:

1. The 9,200 IP addresses were not compromised consumer devices. They were the IP addresses of **9,200 distinct human users**, each of whom had, during the 47-second window, accessed Target-4488-001's customer service portal from their personal computer or phone, on their home or workplace network, on what every available log indicated was a deliberate action initiated by the user.
2. The 9,200 users were geographically distributed across 41 countries, on six continents, with no shared internet service provider, no shared device fingerprint, no shared browser configuration, and no shared user-agent pattern that would suggest a common origin.
3. The 9,200 users had, in the preceding twelve months, performed a combined total of zero searches related to Target-4488-001, its industry, or its activities. None had previously visited the targeted website. None had a customer service history with the company.
4. When sampled and contacted under cover stories, **the 9,200 users had no memory of the visit.** Browser histories confirmed the visits had occurred. The users denied initiating them. They were not lying. The Foundation has verified this across an interview sample of 437 individuals.

The Foundation's working interpretation of the 2017 event, established by 2020, was that an unknown mechanism had, for 47 seconds, caused 9,200 unaware individuals to simultaneously visit a specific corporate website. The visits had no apparent operational consequence — the website experienced a brief load spike and recovered — but the *coordination* was, by Foundation determination, anomalous beyond any conventional explanation.

The Department of Anomalous Group Behaviour was tasked with retrospective analysis. By 2022, it had identified **287 distinct prior events** in the historical record, dating back to 2009, that fit the SCP-4488 signature. The earliest events had populations of 50-100 participants and were below any plausible detection threshold of the time. The events had grown in scale steadily, doubling approximately every 14 months, until the 2017 event crossed the noise threshold of contemporary monitoring infrastructure and became visible.

The growth has continued. The current SCP-4488 population is, by central estimate, approximately seven thousand times the size it was when the phenomenon first became detectable. The Foundation has no reason to believe the growth rate has slowed.

## Addendum 4488-B — Target Pattern

The targets of SCP-4488 coordination events, since identified, have displayed a consistent pattern. Of the 287 events catalogued as of ██/██/20██, target categories are distributed as follows:

- **141 events (49%)** targeted multinational corporations in the fossil fuel, petrochemical, mining, weapons manufacturing, or industrial agriculture sectors.
- **63 events (22%)** targeted private individuals — predominantly chief executives, major shareholders, or board members of corporations in the above sectors, but also including a smaller number of media figures, financial executives, and political figures.
- **41 events (14%)** targeted government agencies or contractors involved in border enforcement, surveillance, or detention operations.
- **22 events (8%)** targeted financial institutions involved in litigation or regulatory disputes related to the above categories.
- **20 events (7%)** targeted entities that the Department of Anomalous Group Behaviour has been unable to classify but which appear, on review, to share structural features with the above (e.g., specific shell companies, holding entities, or supply-chain intermediaries).

The Foundation has, with significant internal debate, characterised this target distribution as **closely matching the target list of various fringe-left political activist groups operating in the same period**. The match is not exact. The Foundation has identified 41 events targeting entities that no known activist group has prominently named. It has also identified a small number of high-profile activist targets that SCP-4488 has, conspicuously, *not* targeted. The pattern is recognisable but is not a copy.

The Foundation considered, at length, whether SCP-4488 might be the work of an organised activist movement employing anomalous methods. This hypothesis was, after eighteen months of investigation, rejected. The reasons are summarised below.

1. **The participants are not activists.** Detailed background investigation of the 8,143 identified PoI-4488-α individuals has established that, as a population, their political affiliations are statistically indistinguishable from regional baselines. Approximately 28% identify as left-leaning, 31% as right-leaning, 22% as centrist or moderate, and the remainder as politically unaffiliated, disengaged, or non-aligned. **The political distribution of PoI-4488-α does not differ significantly from the general population.** Right-leaning participants have been used in coordinated actions against right-leaning targets. Left-leaning participants have been used in coordinated actions against left-leaning targets. None of the participants would, on the basis of their own stated views, choose to participate in the events they have been used in. Many would actively oppose them.

2. **The coordination mechanism is not consistent with human organisation.** Every identified activist group, including those investigated for possible anomalous capabilities, operates with detectable communication infrastructure — even if encrypted, even if highly distributed. SCP-4488 has no such infrastructure. The coordination occurs *without any detectable signal* passing between participants. The Foundation has examined this with state-of-the-art SIGINT capabilities, financial-flow analysis, social-network analysis, and (in three documented cases) deep cover infiltration. There is no message. There is no signal. There is no plan.

3. **The participants have no memory of their actions.** Activists, even highly compartmentalised ones, know they are activists. SCP-4488 participants do not. The unawareness has been verified in interview after interview, with consistency across cultures, languages, and investigation methodologies. The participants are not concealing knowledge. They simply do not have it.

The Foundation has, therefore, classified the target pattern as a **signature** of SCP-4488 rather than a *purpose*. Something or someone is selecting targets along these political lines. The participants are not aware of the selection. The Foundation does not know who or what is doing the selecting. The political reading is, by present evidence, a property of the system's output — not a description of the system's intent.

## Addendum 4488-C — Action Catalogue

The actions performed by PoI-4488-α individuals in coordinated events have, across 287 catalogued incidents, been overwhelmingly mundane. A selection is reproduced below to illustrate the range.

- **Mass website visits.** As described in the 2017 event. Used 162 times. Effects range from minor server load to full denial-of-service depending on participant count and target capacity.
- **Mass account creation.** Coordinated sign-ups for customer service portals, mailing lists, free-trial subscriptions, and loyalty programs at targeted corporations. Used 47 times. Effects include exhaustion of customer service capacity and inflation of marketing databases with apparently genuine but inactive accounts.
- **Mass telephone calls.** Coordinated calls to corporate switchboards, customer service lines, executive direct lines, and (in 14 documented cases) the personal mobile phones of targeted individuals. The participants speak briefly, often pleasantly, sometimes inquiring about a product or service, sometimes asking an open-ended question, sometimes simply saying "hello" and then ending the call. They retain no memory of the calls. Their phone bills confirm the calls were made.
- **Mass physical visits.** Coordinated arrivals at specific physical locations — corporate headquarters, executive residences, retail outlets associated with targeted brands, regulatory hearings, court proceedings. The participants typically do not enter the targeted location. They walk past. They linger briefly in the vicinity. They take a photograph of the building exterior on their phone. They leave. Their movements are, individually, unremarkable. The aggregate effect is a brief, dense pulse of pedestrian traffic at a specific time and place.
- **Mass identical communications.** Sending of structurally similar (but never identical) emails, letters, or social media posts to targeted entities. The communications are typically polite, ostensibly individual, and reference some matter of public concern related to the target. They appear, on inspection, to be authentic constituent or customer communications. The Department of Anomalous Group Behaviour has been unable to identify the source of the underlying template. The participants do not remember composing the messages.
- **Mass searches and trending manipulation.** Coordinated search queries on specific terms relating to a target, or specific identical posts on social media platforms, calibrated to push particular phrases into trending or autocomplete rankings. Used 38 times. The effect is a brief surge of apparent organic public interest in a specific phrase or topic associated with the target.
- **Mass small transactions.** Coordinated micro-purchases or micro-investments — buying a single share of a targeted company's stock, donating $5 to a campaign opposing the target, or in three documented cases, requesting refunds for purchases that had never been made. The financial impact is negligible. The administrative load is non-trivial.

The Foundation has not, in any of the 287 events, identified any action by PoI-4488-α that could be characterised as **violent, threatening, illegal, or directly destructive.** The actions are uniformly within the bounds of normal civilian behaviour. They are, taken individually, the kinds of things one person might do on an ordinary day for ordinary reasons. The anomaly is that tens of thousands of unconnected people are doing them at the same moment, against the same target, for reasons none of them know.

The targets, in many cases, *are aware that something unusual is happening.* Several of the affected corporations have, over the past five years, contracted private security and cybersecurity firms to investigate what they describe as "coordinated harassment campaigns" or "low-grade hybrid attacks." None of these investigations have, to the Foundation's knowledge, identified the true cause. Two have produced reports attributing the activity to specific named activist organisations, who have denied involvement. The denials are, in those cases, accurate.

## Addendum 4488-D — Why PoI-4488-α Are Not Contacted

The Foundation's standing policy of non-contact with identified PoI-4488-α individuals was established in 2021 following extensive Ethics Committee deliberation. The reasoning is summarised below for the record.

The PoI-4488-α population, on every measure available to the Foundation, consists of **innocent civilians who are being used without their knowledge or consent.** They have committed no crime. They hold no anomalous belief. They have, by all available evidence, no connection to anything anomalous *except* their involuntary participation in SCP-4488. They are, in every meaningful sense, **victims** of the phenomenon, not its agents.

The arguments for contact are operational: a participant who knew of their status might be able to provide useful information, might be able to consciously resist the next coordination event, or might be able to recall fragments of the recruitment or activation process. The arguments against contact are ethical and practical:

1. The participants did not choose this. Informing them would impose, without their consent, knowledge that they have been used in actions they did not condone, against targets they may personally support, by a mechanism they cannot understand. The Foundation has determined, after psychological consultation, that this disclosure would produce significant distress in the average participant and severe distress in a non-trivial minority.
2. The Foundation cannot offer any meaningful relief. There is no known method to remove a participant from PoI-4488-α. There is no known method to verify that a removal has occurred. There is no known method to prevent future recruitment. Disclosure would inform a victim of a violation they cannot escape.
3. There is no operational evidence that informing participants would produce useful information. The 437 individuals interviewed under cover stories were asked, in various indirect ways, whether they had any memory of, intuition about, or unexplained associations with the events they had been used in. None did. The information is, by Foundation determination, not retrievable through participant interviews.
4. There is a non-zero risk that disclosure would itself constitute a vector. The Foundation cannot rule out the possibility that SCP-4488's recruitment mechanism operates on individuals who become aware of the phenomenon. The exposure of a current participant to detailed knowledge of their participation might trigger an unknown effect — including, in the worst case, the recruitment of the informed individual into a different or expanded role.

The Ethics Committee's recommendation, accepted by O5 vote in 2021 and reaffirmed in 2024, is therefore that the Foundation **observe, mitigate, and investigate** without informing the affected population. The recommendation acknowledges, in its concluding paragraph, that this is a choice the Foundation has made to prioritise its investigative interests over the affected population's right to know what has been done to them. The recommendation classifies this choice as a *known ethical compromise* and requires that it be reviewed annually.

The most recent review, dated ██/██/2025, upheld the policy.

The minority dissenting opinion, attached to the most recent review and authored by Dr. K█████ (Department of Resilient Cognition), notes the following:

> *We have now sustained, for four years, a policy of non-disclosure to a population of victims who are larger than the population of the city in which I am writing this opinion. We have sustained it for the most defensible of reasons. We have also, in that time, observed the population grow by approximately a factor of four. At some point, the number of people we are not telling will exceed our capacity to maintain the cover. At some point, one of them will figure it out. We should consider whether we would prefer that disclosure to happen by our design or by accident.*
>
> *I do not have a proposal. I have only the concern. I am filing it for the record.*

The concern has been filed for the record.

## Addendum 4488-E — Hypothesis Space

The Department of Anomalous Group Behaviour maintains a formal hypothesis space describing possible mechanisms underlying SCP-4488. The hypotheses are evaluated periodically against new evidence. None have been confirmed. None have been definitively ruled out. The current ranked hypothesis space, in descending order of present plausibility, is reproduced below.

**Hypothesis 1: SCP-5829-Ω Variant.** SCP-4488 represents a downstream effect or specialised variant of SCP-5829-Ω, the memetic agent described in SCP-5829's documentation. Under this hypothesis, PoI-4488-α individuals are infected with a Stage-4 or Stage-5 SCP-5829-Ω instance that has developed a coordination function — possibly an emergent one, possibly directed by an underlying intent that the Foundation has not identified.

*Evidence for:* Both phenomena involve unwilling biological hosts performing actions outside their conscious awareness. Both display growth patterns consistent with infectious propagation. Both originate or became detectable in the same general historical window. SCP-5829 has documented variants capable of inter-host coordination.

*Evidence against:* Foundation screening of identified PoI-4488-α individuals for SCP-5829 markers has returned **zero positive results** across an 8,143-person sample. SCP-4488 participants do not display any of the Stage-3 or Stage-4 SCP-5829 symptoms (behavioural drift, evangelism, propagation of memetic content). SCP-4488 actions are mechanistically dissimilar from SCP-5829's known propagation patterns.

*Current status:* **Cannot be confirmed or ruled out.** Possible if SCP-4488 represents a variant that has evaded current screening methods. The Foundation has commissioned the development of next-generation screening tools, with completion projected for ██/2027.

---

**Hypothesis 2: Independent Anomaly.** SCP-4488 is a distinct anomaly with no causal connection to SCP-5829, sharing surface features by coincidence. The phenomena emerged in the same historical window because that window has, in general, seen an increase in the rate of memetic and group-behavioural anomalies (cf. the 2009 caseload inflection described in SCP-2001 documentation).

*Evidence for:* The mechanistic differences between SCP-4488 and SCP-5829 are substantial. PoI-4488-α individuals are entirely unaware of their participation, while SCP-5829 hosts at Stage 4+ retain awareness and, in some cases, become evangelists. The action profile is different. The screening results support the distinction.

*Evidence against:* The growth-rate and target-selection patterns of SCP-4488 are difficult to explain without an organising intent. Independent emergence of a phenomenon this internally coherent, this targeted, and this resistant to investigation is — while not impossible — unparsimonious.

*Current status:* **Plausible but unsatisfying.** Treated as the null hypothesis against which other hypotheses are evaluated.

---

**Hypothesis 3: Adversarial Group of Interest.** SCP-4488 is the work of an unidentified Group of Interest deploying anomalous capabilities to conduct a covert influence operation. The target selection reflects the political agenda of the controlling entity.

*Evidence for:* The targeting is too consistent and too sophisticated to be unintended. The political signature, while not perfectly aligned with any single known activist organisation, is recognisable enough to suggest deliberate selection rather than emergent pattern.

*Evidence against:* The Foundation has not identified any Group of Interest, known or suspected, with anomalous capabilities adequate to this scale. The growth rate of SCP-4488 over 16 years would require a level of sustained operational capacity that no GoI has previously demonstrated. The recruitment of politically heterogeneous participants is operationally counterproductive — a deliberate actor would, presumably, prefer politically aligned recruits.

*Current status:* **Persistent low-probability candidate.** Investigated periodically. No GoI has been identified.

---

**Hypothesis 4: Pre-existing Substrate.** SCP-4488 is not a recent phenomenon. The mechanism by which large populations can be unconsciously coordinated has existed throughout human history and has been used many times before, by many different agencies, for many different purposes. The Foundation is detecting it now because the post-internet world produces signatures (digital coordination, financial records, geolocation data) that historical instances did not. The current target pattern reflects the current operator's politics; the underlying mechanism is older and politically neutral.

*Evidence for:* Historical analysis has identified a number of unexplained mass-behaviour events — religious revivals, sudden migration waves, simultaneous adoption of cultural practices across non-communicating populations — that fit the SCP-4488 signature when retroactively examined. The growth rate may reflect not the phenomenon's growth but the Foundation's improving ability to see it.

*Evidence against:* The historical events identified are individually disputable and could be explained through conventional sociological mechanisms (cultural diffusion, parallel response to shared stimuli, etc.). The current detection capability has improved substantially since 2009 but probably not by a factor of seven thousand.

*Current status:* **Increasingly interesting.** Has gained support within the Department of Anomalous Group Behaviour over the past three years. If correct, the implications extend far beyond SCP-4488's current political signature.

---

**Hypothesis 5: Unknown.** Something is happening that the Foundation has not yet conceived of. This category exists because, in the Department's own assessment, the above four hypotheses do not feel like the complete space.

*Evidence for:* The phenomenon has persisted for over a decade of investigation without yielding to any of the above frameworks.

*Evidence against:* Not falsifiable.

*Current status:* **Maintained as a placeholder.** No active investigation. The Department's standing instruction is to be alert to evidence that does not fit Hypotheses 1-4 and to report it immediately.

## Addendum 4488-F — A Note from the Department

The following memo was attached, without further comment, to the most recent quarterly report of the Department of Anomalous Group Behaviour. It is reproduced here at the Department Director's request.

> *I want to note, for whatever record this will eventually constitute, that the people in SCP-4488 are not the bad guys. They are not even the good guys. They are just people. They have jobs and families and political views and reasons for holding those views, and most of them have, somewhere in their lives, been on a website's loading screen wondering why the page was so slow, and that loading screen was, on at least one occasion, slow because they were on it. They did not know. They went on with their day. They will, statistically, go on with many more days. We are watching them.*
>
> *We are watching them because we have to. We are watching them because something is using them, and we would like to know what, and we would like, if possible, to make it stop, so that they can have the dignity of being only the people they thought they were.*
>
> *I want to record, for the institutional memory of the Department, that this is what we are doing. Not stopping a threat. Not containing a danger. Helping, in a way our subjects do not know we are helping, a population of unknowing victims, against an attacker we cannot find, on behalf of targets we are not, in many cases, particularly fond of. The political signature of our work is, in this matter, entirely incidental. Our actual position is the position the Foundation has held in many other matters: someone is being used; we would like that to stop.*
>
> *I think it is important that we say this out loud, occasionally, in writing, so that the people who come after us do not mistake us for the people the targets think we are.*
