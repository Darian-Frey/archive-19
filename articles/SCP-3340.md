# SCP-3340

**Item #:** SCP-3340

**Object Class:** Euclid

**Special Containment Procedures:** SCP-3340 is a 2.4-mile stretch of public road designated as the **B████** in ████████shire, England, between the villages of ████████ and ████████-on-the-████. The road is open to ordinary public traffic and is not anomalous in any visible respect. Containment procedures address the *concealment* of SCP-3340's anomalous properties rather than the road itself.

The Foundation maintains a passive observation post in a converted barn approximately 300 metres from the road's midpoint. The observation post is staffed by two embedded agents posing as the property's owners — a retired couple who keep chickens and occasionally rent a holiday cottage on the adjacent paddock. The cover has been in place since 2014 and is regarded as durable.

All UK Ordnance Survey, satellite-navigation, and routing-software datasets referencing the B████ are subject to Foundation-administered data sanitisation. The published transit-time figures for the road are derived from the slower (3340-South) direction only and are presented as symmetric, effectively concealing the existence of the asymmetry by overstating the northbound transit time by approximately 16 seconds. The discrepancy is small enough that individual motorists comparing their journey times to the official figures attribute the difference to traffic, speed compliance, or instrument variance, and do not pursue it. This concealment is maintained via embedded assets at the relevant institutions and has, to date, not been independently identified by any third party.

The civilian discoverer of SCP-3340, PoI-3340 (**Mr. Geoffrey H██████**, retired surveyor, of ████████), is under Class-C monitoring and remains in correspondence with the Foundation under a cover identity (a representative of the "Royal Society for Geographic Curiosities," a fictitious learned society). Mr. H██████ believes his findings are of academic interest only and has been content to publish them in an internal newsletter of a society he does not know does not exist. He receives a quarterly stipend and a Christmas hamper. He is, by all available measures, delighted.

---

**Description:** SCP-3340 is a 2.4-mile section of the B████, a minor B-road in rural England, which exhibits **directional temporal asymmetry**: travel along the road in one direction (designated, by Foundation convention, **3340-North**) requires a measurably shorter duration of proper time than travel in the opposite direction (**3340-South**), for the same distance, at the same velocity, by the same vehicle, under identical environmental conditions.

The asymmetry is distributed along the length of the road. The 3340-North transit accumulates a deficit of approximately **0.42 seconds per 100 metres** of distance travelled, relative to the 3340-South transit. Over the full 2.4-mile (3,862-metre) length of the affected section, this produces a total differential of approximately **16.2 seconds per traversal**. The effect is reproducible to four decimal places, has been verified by multiple independent measurement methods (cesium clock, GPS triangulation, mechanical stopwatch averaged across 10,000 trials), and **does not depend on the speed of the vehicle**, the time of day, the weather, the season, the year, the vehicle's mass, or the consciousness or awareness of the driver. The per-distance differential is invariant; the total differential per traversal depends only on the distance covered within the affected zone.

The effect is, by every test the Foundation has been able to apply, **a genuine local violation of the temporal symmetry of physical law.** It is not a measurement artefact. It is not a result of road surface differences, atmospheric pressure gradients, gravitational anomalies, magnetic anomalies, or any other identifiable physical cause. The two ends of the road are at the same elevation to within 0.3 metres. The road is, in every respect that the Foundation has been able to measure, ordinary.

For ordinary motorists travelling the road at posted speeds, the 16.2-second differential is small enough to fall below the threshold of conscious perception — a transit that should take approximately 4 minutes 36 seconds southbound takes approximately 4 minutes 20 seconds northbound, a difference well within the natural variation produced by traffic, road conditions, and driver behaviour. The effect is, in practical terms, invisible to anyone not measuring it deliberately. This is, the Foundation has come to believe, the reason it has gone undetected for as long as it has.

Time, on the B████, runs measurably faster in one direction than the other, by a margin that has, since the road's earliest known use, gone unremarked by every traveller who has not happened to be timing themselves with a precision instrument. The Foundation has been unable to explain this. The Foundation has, after eleven years of investigation, stopped expecting to.

## Addendum 3340-A — Discovery

SCP-3340 was identified in 2014 by Mr. Geoffrey H██████, a retired land surveyor and amateur enthusiast of British road history. Mr. H██████ had, for approximately fifteen years prior to discovery, been engaged in a self-funded project to produce what he described in a private letter as "a definitive transit-time atlas of the minor roads of the West Country," motivated by a personal observation that "the official figures are slapdash and reflect very little real driving."

Mr. H██████'s methodology consisted of driving each road in his survey at the posted speed limit, at low traffic density, in both directions, on multiple occasions, while recording his transit times with a high-precision stopwatch he had purchased at an estate sale. He had completed approximately 1,400 such measurements when he first observed the B████ asymmetry.

In a letter to a friend (since recovered and archived), Mr. H██████ described his initial reaction:

> *I have driven that road forty-six times, twenty-three in each direction. The northbound transits are consistently shorter than the southbound transits by an average of sixteen seconds, give or take. I have checked the stopwatch against the kitchen clock. I have checked the speedometer. I have driven the road at the same speed in both directions, holding 50 by the needle the whole way, and the difference persists. I am, at present, completely unable to think of a reason for this. I have, I confess, become slightly preoccupied.*

Mr. H██████ continued his investigation for approximately fourteen months before publishing his findings in a 6-page article in the newsletter of the local historical society. The article was titled *"A Curious Asymmetry on the B████: A Surveyor's Note."* It received three published responses, two from sceptics suggesting measurement error and one from a retired physics teacher who, on reading the article, performed his own measurements and confirmed the effect.

The retired physics teacher was the source of Foundation awareness. He had, in 1971, briefly worked as a Foundation contractor on an unrelated calibration project, and retained — in violation of his nondisclosure agreement, though by this point the Foundation was sympathetic — a Foundation contact address. He wrote to the address.

A field agent was dispatched. Mr. H██████ was contacted under the Royal Society for Geographic Curiosities cover, his findings were "purchased" for the Society's archive, and he was warmly congratulated on his discovery. He was also, with his agreement, asked not to publish further. He has not. The Foundation considers him one of its most pleasant and accommodating Persons of Interest.

## Addendum 3340-B — Characterisation

Foundation measurement of SCP-3340 between 2014 and 2017 established the effect's properties in detail. The properties are reproduced in summary form below.

**1. Directionality.** The faster direction (3340-North) corresponds to travel from grid reference ██/██ to grid reference ██/██. The slower direction (3340-South) is the reverse. The two directions are, in every respect *other than transit time*, identical — the same road, the same surface, the same posted speed limit, the same width, the same elevation profile.

**2. Magnitude.** The differential is **0.42 seconds per 100 metres of road traversed**, with a standard error of less than 0.001 seconds per 100m across 10,000 measured traversals. The per-distance rate is constant. It does not scale with velocity, mass, or duration. A walker covering the road in 45 minutes experiences the same per-distance differential as a vehicle covering it in three minutes; both accumulate approximately 16.2 seconds of total differential across the full 2.4-mile span. The differential accumulates linearly with distance travelled within the affected zone and does not accumulate at all outside it.

**3. Locality.** The effect is bounded. It begins and ends at specific points on the road, identified by the Foundation to within 5 metres. The endpoints are not marked. There is no visible feature at either endpoint. The northern endpoint is approximately 80 metres past a roadside bench. The southern endpoint is approximately 15 metres past a stone wall belonging to a sheep paddock. The wall is approximately 200 years old. The bench is approximately 30 years old. Neither structure is anomalous.

**4. Universality.** The effect applies to *all* matter traversing the road, including pedestrians, vehicles, animals, and — confirmed in a single experiment — a small radioactive sample whose decay rate was used as an internal clock. The radioactive sample, after one 3340-North traversal, had decayed slightly less than control samples traversing the same distance off-road, by an amount precisely consistent with 16.2 seconds of "missing" time.

**5. Conservation.** The Foundation initially expected that the per-traversal differential would be compensated somewhere — that whatever "saves" time on the northbound journey must "pay" it back on the southbound journey, or in some adjacent region. **No such compensation has been detected.** Vehicles, persons, and decay samples making the round trip experience a *net loss* of approximately 32.4 seconds compared to a control journey of equivalent distance. The time is gone. It is not stored anywhere the Foundation has been able to locate. It does not appear in adjacent regions, in subsequent durations, or in the road itself.

The conservation failure is, to Foundation physicists, the most disturbing property of the anomaly. Time dilation, in conventional physics, is symmetric or compensated. SCP-3340 is neither. It is a small, ongoing, unbalanced removal of duration from the universe, occurring every time anyone travels along a particular two-mile stretch of road in ████████shire.

The Foundation has, with considerable methodological caution, attempted to estimate the cumulative time loss across the road's history. The estimate depends sensitively on assumed traffic volumes, which themselves vary across centuries (foot traffic in the medieval period, cart traffic in the early modern period, motor traffic since approximately 1920). Under the Foundation's central traffic-history model, and assuming a road age of at least 1,200 years, the cumulative proper time removed from the universe via the B████ alone is on the order of **several decades** — though the uncertainty band is wide and the figure could plausibly be considerably higher. The Foundation does not know where the time has gone. The Foundation does not know whether it has gone anywhere, or whether "gone" is the right framework.

These figures apply only to the B████. The corresponding figure for the wider network (cf. Addendum 3340-C) is, by the same methodology, on the order of **several millennia of cumulative proper time** since the network's apparent date of origin. The Foundation has elected not to publish this figure beyond the immediate research team. It does not appear to make any of the senior physicists feel better to dwell on it.

## Addendum 3340-C — The Network

In 2018, following the characterisation of SCP-3340, the Foundation's Department of Anomalous Geography initiated a systematic survey of similar minor roads across the United Kingdom, in the hope of identifying any other instances. The project was internally referred to as **the H██████ Survey**, in honour of its originator, and was expected to be a thorough but ultimately fruitless exercise.

By the end of 2019, the survey had identified **forty-seven** additional roads exhibiting comparable directional time asymmetry.

By the end of 2022, the count was **two hundred and eighty-three**, distributed throughout England, Wales, Scotland, and Northern Ireland, with a marked concentration in regions of long-standing rural habitation. By 2024, preliminary surveys conducted by Foundation assets in continental Europe had identified at least sixty more instances in France, the Netherlands, Germany, and Italy.

The roads are not visibly distinguishable from non-anomalous roads of similar age and type. They are typically minor B-roads or unclassified lanes, often connecting two villages or a village and an outlying hamlet, in regions where the modern road follows a route of considerable antiquity. The differential magnitudes vary between instances, from approximately **0.05 seconds per 100 metres** (the smallest detected) to **1.4 seconds per 100 metres** (the largest), but **the per-distance rate is fixed per instance** — every road has its own constant differential rate, which does not vary with conditions. The total per-traversal differential therefore depends on the length of each affected section, ranging from a few seconds on the shortest instances to over a minute on the longest.

The directionality of the differentials, when plotted on a map of the United Kingdom, **does not point at any single location.** The "faster" directions form a complex pattern with no obvious centre. The Foundation initially expected the pattern would resolve into a hub, a vector, or a flow — some structure suggesting purpose. It does not. The pattern is, at the scale of the British Isles, **statistically isotropic.** The differentials do not converge or diverge. They simply exist, in a distribution that is uniform across all measurable axes.

The Foundation's working hypothesis, advanced by Dr. C█████████ in 2023, is that the roads are not points on a flow but **points on a balance**. The total "lost" time across all instances of SCP-3340 in any sufficiently large region is, when measured, very close to zero — the asymmetries cancel, in aggregate, across the network. The local violation is real, but the global system is conservative. The Foundation is in the position of having discovered the local cells of a machine whose function is to maintain an exact temporal equilibrium across the surface of a country, while extracting from each individual location a small, unbalanced toll.

What the machine is doing with the local imbalances, or why it requires individual cells at all when the global sum is zero, the Foundation has not determined.

Dr. C█████████'s informal characterisation, recorded in a meeting that was not minuted, was: *"It is doing something. We are not what it is doing it for. We are the corridor it does it in."*

## Addendum 3340-D — Historical Layers

In 2020, the Foundation commissioned a historical-cartographic analysis of the 283 identified SCP-3340 instances, comparing their routes against known historical road networks dating back to the Romano-British period.

The results are summarised below.

- **94%** of identified SCP-3340 roads follow routes that are demonstrably pre-modern, i.e., the route existed in some form before AD 1500.
- **71%** follow routes that are demonstrably medieval or earlier.
- **41%** follow routes that can be reliably identified in Domesday-era (11th century) sources or earlier.
- **18%** follow routes that overlay or closely parallel known Roman roads.
- **6%** follow routes that, on archaeological evidence, predate the Roman occupation entirely, corresponding to Iron Age or Bronze Age trackways.

The oldest confirmed SCP-3340 instance, designated **SCP-3340-187**, follows a 1.6-mile section of an unclassified country lane in ██████shire. The lane closely tracks an ancient trackway whose earliest dated archaeological evidence places it at **circa 2400 BC** — approximately 4,400 years before the present.

The implication, which the Foundation has not formally adopted but which is increasingly difficult to avoid, is that the SCP-3340 network is **not a modern phenomenon imposed on existing roads.** It is the *original feature.* The roads were built — or worn into the landscape by foot traffic — along routes that already had the anomalous property. The asymmetry is older than the road. The road is the consequence of the asymmetry: human and animal traffic, given a choice between two equivalent routes, would unconsciously prefer the one that took less time to traverse, and would gradually wear in a path along it.

The SCP-3340 network is not on the roads. The roads are on the network. **Human civilisation in the British Isles has, for at least four millennia, been quietly built along the lines of a system whose purpose it has never known.**

This finding has not been formally communicated to UK governmental authorities. The Department of Anomalous Geography, in consultation with the Ethics Committee, determined that the disclosure would produce significant disruption to public infrastructure planning while providing no actionable benefit, since the Foundation has identified no means by which the network can be modified, disrupted, or used. The Foundation has, accordingly, chosen to know quietly.

## Addendum 3340-E — The Toll

The Foundation has, since the network's identification, conducted a series of small-scale experiments to determine whether the cumulative time loss has any detectable effect on persons who traverse SCP-3340 instances regularly.

The results are described below.

**1. Personnel-level effect.** Foundation personnel who commute regularly along a single SCP-3340 instance — typically as part of the embedded observation cover — experience cumulative proper time loss on the order of **two to four hours per year**, depending on the instance and the commute frequency. Over a 30-year working life, the cumulative loss is between approximately 2.5 and 5 days. This is, the Department of Anomalous Geography has noted, *not* operationally negligible. The Foundation has, since 2019, rotated personnel through SCP-3340 observation posts on five-year cycles rather than indefinite postings, in order to limit individual exposure.

For long-term residents of villages adjacent to SCP-3340 instances — who may traverse the affected road multiple times daily across a lifetime — the cumulative figures are considerably larger. A resident of ████████ who has lived in the village from birth and commutes daily to ████████-on-the-████ for a working life could, in principle, have lost on the order of **several weeks** of proper time across their lifetime. This figure is, on its face, large enough that it should produce detectable consequences.

**2. Community-level effect.** It does not. Rural communities located near SCP-3340 instances, particularly those whose residents traverse the affected road as part of routine daily life, show **no detectable demographic anomaly** — no statistical deviation in life expectancy, no developmental abnormalities, no recorded cultural artefacts suggesting awareness of the effect.

This is, in itself, deeply surprising. Given the magnitudes involved, the Foundation expected that the cumulative effect would produce *some* detectable demographic signature. It has not. The Foundation's current working hypothesis is that the time loss is not "subtracted" from the affected person's lifespan or experience in any conventional sense — it is, instead, **subtracted from the universe at the moment of traversal**, in a manner that does not propagate forward into the person's subsequent existence. The traveller is not shortened. The journey is shortened. The journey ends. The traveller continues, in every respect, as if it had not.

This is, by Foundation physics, **not a thing time is supposed to be able to do.**

**3. Cultural-trace effect.** Folklore collected from regions adjacent to the four oldest confirmed SCP-3340 instances includes, in three of four cases, **a local belief or saying** referring to one direction of the road as "the short way" or "the good way," and the other as "the long way" or "the wrong way." The beliefs are not consistent in their explanations — one is attributed to fairies, one to the local saint, one to the wind, one to "the way the road wants to go." The beliefs are old. They are, by all available evidence, **right.**

Local residents in the affected communities, when interviewed informally by Foundation field agents posing as folklorists, have, on multiple occasions, asserted that they preferentially use the "fast" direction when in a hurry and the "slow" direction when they are not. They do not, in any case examined, articulate a reason. They simply say that one direction is for some kinds of journey and the other is for other kinds.

The Foundation has not, to date, encountered a local resident who has consciously identified the asymmetry as anomalous. The asymmetry has been folded, across centuries, into the unconscious geography of the communities that live alongside it. They use it correctly. They do not notice they are using it.

## Addendum 3340-F — The Unanswered Question

The Foundation has, after eleven years of investigation, identified the following facts:

- A network of approximately 350 known SCP-3340 instances exists across the United Kingdom and continental Europe.
- The network is at least 4,400 years old, and probably older.
- The network has been continuously maintained — that is, the asymmetries have not decayed, drifted, or rotated, over millennia.
- The network is conservative in aggregate but locally unbalanced.
- The network has been used, knowingly or unknowingly, by every human civilisation that has occupied the affected regions.
- The network does not appear to have any *purpose* the Foundation can identify. It does not transport, store, signal, or compute. It simply *is*, and has been, for longer than human writing has existed.

The Foundation has, with significant internal disagreement, accepted that the most operationally honest characterisation of SCP-3340 is the following:

> *There is a machine in the landscape. We do not know what it does. We do not know who built it. We do not know whether anyone built it, or whether it grew. We know that it has been running, without fail, for at least four millennia, that it requires no apparent maintenance, and that it processes the duration of every journey along several hundred specific routes in our country.*
>
> *It does not seem to care about us. It does not appear to be malevolent. It is also not, by any of the standards we ordinarily use, designed for us. We are using it without permission. It has not objected. It has not, by any test we can devise, noticed.*
>
> *We are recommending no action. We are recommending that the Foundation document the network, monitor for changes, and otherwise allow the system to continue functioning as it has done since prehistory. We are doing this because we do not have a better option. We are also doing it because, on reflection, we are not entirely sure we want to interfere with a machine that has been quietly removing proper time from the universe at a precise per-distance rate for forty centuries and is, by every available metric, still working perfectly.*
>
> *We do not know what would happen if we broke it. We do not propose to find out.*

This recommendation was accepted by O5 vote in 2024 and is current Foundation policy.

## Addendum 3340-G — Mr. H██████

Mr. Geoffrey H██████ celebrated his 79th birthday in ██/2025. The Foundation, via the Royal Society for Geographic Curiosities, sent him a card, a bottle of Tawny Port, and a framed certificate naming him as the Society's "Discoverer of the Year, Honorary, in Perpetuity." He was reportedly very pleased.

He continues to drive the B████, occasionally, when he visits his daughter in ████████-on-the-████. He has not, by Foundation observation, ever taken the "slow" direction when in a hurry. He has not, by his own account, ever consciously thought about this.

The Foundation has chosen not to inform him.

He has earned, the Department of Anomalous Geography has noted, a quiet retirement.
